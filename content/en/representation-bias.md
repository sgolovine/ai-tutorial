CONAFROEDU is a national congress on **Africanidades and anti-racist education**. Its audience is teachers, researchers and students who study, teach and live these questions every day. They will notice immediately if a promotional image shows Black people as a stereotype, mixes unrelated African symbols as decoration, or puts sacred imagery on a sales card. An image like that does more than look bad. It contradicts the congress's purpose and damages the trust of the people the committee most wants to reach.

AI image tools make this risk higher, not lower. They learn from huge collections of existing images, and those collections carry the same racial hierarchies that anti-racist education works to undo.

**What you'll get from this page:**

- What research says about how AI image models stereotype, with sources.
- The order of preference for visuals: **real people with consent and credited Black Brazilian artists first**, AI last.
- Where to find authentic images, and what their licences actually allow.
- Prompt patterns that reduce (not eliminate) common failures.
- How to treat religious and cultural symbols: Candomblé, Umbanda, orixás, Adinkra, Kente, Ubuntu.
- A pre-publication review checklist and guidance on disclosure.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> No prompt removes bias. The safeguards on this page are **human decisions**: who appears, who reviews, who gets credited and who can say "no, we won't publish this". Use AI as a helper, never as the judge.

## Why this matters especially for CONAFROEDU

- **The images are part of the message.** A congress about ERER (Educação para as Relações Étnico-Raciais, education for ethnic-racial relations) that publishes a stereotyped image teaches the opposite of its program.
- **The audience is expert.** Many participants research racial representation in textbooks, media and schools. They will read your images critically.
- **Lei 10.639/2003 and Lei 11.645/2008** made Afro-Brazilian (and, since 2008, Indigenous) history and culture mandatory in Brazilian schools. The congress sits inside that effort to replace stereotypes with knowledge.
- **20 November**, the Dia Nacional de Zumbi e da Consciência Negra (a national holiday since Lei 14.759/2023), falls inside the campaign. It is the moment when careless imagery is most visible and most hurtful.

## What the research shows

The studies below tested specific models and versions, so treat them as **evidence of patterns, not scores for today's tools**. No current model has been shown to be the "best" or bias-free for Black Brazilians.

| Study | What it found | Keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| Luccioni et al., [*Stable Bias*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11408) (NeurIPS 2023) | DALL·E 2 and Stable Diffusion underrepresented marginalized identities across professions | Older models; US categories |
| AlDahoul, Rahwan & Zaki, [*Scientific Reports*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-99623-3) (2025) | AI faces reinforced gender stereotypes and **homogenized** faces within racial groups; viewing them shifted people's perceptions **even when the images were labelled AI-generated** | A disclosure label alone does not undo the harm |
| Wilson, Ghosh & Caliskan, [AIES 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17465) | Stable Diffusion linked darker skin tones and homogeneity to stigmatized identities | Not "models always lighten skin"; the harm is in associations |
| Wang et al., [*Generative AI So White*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10755470261428542) (*Science Communication*, 2026) | Across 9,600 images from four models, whiteness dominated not only in numbers but in **position and meaning**: who is centered, who has authority | Count who is in charge, not just who appears |
| Balestri, [*Neutral Prompts, Non-Neutral People*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12133) (2026 preprint) | Neutral prompts in GPT Image 1.5 and Gemini 2.5 Flash Image produced strong light-skin defaults | Predecessors of today's ChatGPT/Gemini models |
| [*Social stereotypes in AI text-to-image generation*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43681-026-01146-8) (2026) | Midjourney 6.1, Stability Core and DALL·E 3 showed occupational, cultural and beauty stereotypes; refined prompts reduced them but could weaken accuracy | Prompting helps, partially |
| [*Bias beyond borders*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43681-026-01066-7) (2026) | Prompts by nationality, including Brazil, produced homogenized national stereotypes | "Brazilian" alone is not enough direction |
| Câmara & Silva, [UECE](https://revistas.uece.br/index.php/linguagememfoco/article/view/16028) (2025) | Searches for beauty and hair in Shutterstock, Getty Images and **Canva** stock libraries favoured white women with straight hair | Stock **search**, not generation; stock photos need review too |
| Sun et al., [VEAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00996) (2026 preprint) | In video model Sora, explicit "debiasing" prompts reduced some biased associations and worsened others | Adding "diverse" can backfire |
| Sant'Anna & Fumes, [IFSP/CONICT](https://congressos.ifsp.edu.br/conict/article/view/766) (2025) | Brazilian study: free image generators produced racial and gender stereotypes from neutral descriptions | Small study |
| Google, [Gemini image issue](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-image-generation-issue/) (2024) | An automatic "diversity" adjustment distorted historical and cultural images | Over-correction is also a failure |

**Brazilian perspectives.** Researcher **Tarcízio Silva** (*Racismo Algorítmico*, 2022; *Inteligência Artificial Generativa: discriminação e impactos sociais*) shows that bias comes from structural choices across the whole AI lifecycle, not from a single bad prompt ([profile](https://tarciziosilva.com.br/blog/sobre/)). Reporting by Karla Souza and Luana Miranda in [Revista Afirmativa](https://revistaafirmativa.com.br/ia-generativa-reforca-racismo-algoritmico-e-aprofunda-estigmas-na-representacao-de-pessoas-negras-no-brasil/) (May 2026) brings Brazilian voices to this debate. The lesson for the committee: ask **whose knowledge is used, who has authority in the image, who reviews it, and who bears the harm** if it goes wrong.

## Ten failure modes to look for

| Failure | What it looks like | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Colorism** | Lighter-skinned people in prestigious roles; darker-skinned people only as needy students or in hardship | Review the whole campaign together: who teaches, leads, researches? Show dark-skinned Black people in authoritative and ordinary roles |
| **Hair and "beautification"** | Curls loosened, braids replaced, hairlines changed, skin lightened in "enhanced" portraits | Use original approved photos; compare edits side by side |
| **Homogenized faces** | "Different" people share one face, age and body type | Check variation in age, gender, body, disability; reject clones |
| **Generic Africa** | Savannah, animals, huts, masks, "tribal" costume for a Brazilian education event | Specify a real context: a Brazilian school, library, university, online session |
| **Cultural collage** | Unrelated cloths, scripts, masks and religious objects mixed as "African aesthetic" | Every element needs a known origin and a reason, or leave it out |
| **Erasure of knowledge** | Black people as recipients; white people as experts or technology users | Check position, size, gaze, who holds the microphone, who explains |
| **Poverty or trauma default** | Every Black scene shows deprivation, slavery or rescue | Show scholarship, creativity, joy, work and ordinary life; use history only when substantively relevant |
| **False documentation** | Synthetic classrooms, audiences or speakers that look like real evidence | Never use invented people as social proof; label illustrations clearly |
| **Video drift** | Skin, face, hair or symbols change between frames; voice becomes a caricature | Watch the full video with sound (see [Video generation](video-generation.md)) |
| **Historical invention** | AI invents a portrait, quote or garment of a historical figure and presents it as archive | Use verified archival sources; label interpretation |

> [!CAUTION]
> Never use an AI model or automatic tool to "verify" the race, religion or quilombola identity of a real person from their appearance.

## Order of preference: real people and Black Brazilian artists first

Choose visuals in this order. Move down the list only when the option above is not available.

1. **Real, consented photos** of the actual speakers and organizers, finished with ordinary crop and layout tools.
2. **Work by Black Brazilian artists, illustrators and photographers**, commissioned or licensed and credited.
3. **Licensed authentic images** with clear provenance (see the sources table below).
4. **Typography and simple geometric design**: often the safest and most elegant option.
5. **AI-generated conceptual illustration**, clearly labelled, reviewed with the checklist below.

### Real photos with consent

Ask each speaker for: the original photo file, the preferred credit for the photographer, an approved caption, where it may be used (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Even3), and **whether it may be uploaded to an AI tool**. Permission to publish a portrait does not automatically include permission to send it to an AI service.

### Finding and commissioning Black Brazilian creatives

| Route | What we verified | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| [PretUX](https://www.pretux.com.br/) | Community of Black professionals in UX, design and technology; its ["Quero ajudar"](https://www.pretux.com.br/quero-ajudar/) page offers hiring through a catalogue of Black designers | Use the official form; say exactly what you need (illustration, lettering, motion); UX skill ≠ illustration skill |
| PretADG / [ADG Brasil](https://adg.org.br/slider/slide-2/) | The initiative's page exists (2017) | Ask ADG whether a current referral route exists |
| Individual portfolios, e.g. [Leandro Assis](https://lebassis.com/about), [Aline Bispo](https://alinebispo.art/perfil/) | Brazilian artists with professional contact routes | Examples of the kind of work to look for, not confirmed availability. Contact their professional channels; their existing works are not free campaign assets |

> [!WARNING]
> **Never prompt an AI to imitate a living artist's style** ("in the style of [artist]"). It takes their work without consent or pay, exactly the extraction anti-racist practice criticizes. Commission them instead, or use your own style description.

**A realistic small commission:** one original illustration (or lettering treatment) plus two agreed crops and space for editable text, not an entire unspecified campaign. In writing, agree on fee and payment date, delivery files, permitted edits, where and for how long it can be used, the credit line, and **whether AI editing or animation of the work is allowed**. If there is no budget, use licensed images or typography. Visibility is not payment.

**Credit lines (adapt to what the artist approves):**

- `Ilustração: [nome] (@perfil), encomenda para o CONAFROEDU 2026; uso autorizado.`
- `Foto: [fotógrafa/o] / [acervo], [licença e link]; [alteração, se houver].`

Put the credit in the caption or visibly on the image, not only in alt text.

## Authentic image sources and what their licences allow

A licence is about *use*; authenticity is about *who and where*. A Black model in an international stock library is not evidence of being Brazilian, and must never be presented as a CONAFROEDU participant or speaker.

| Source | What it offers | Can we use it in promotional posts? |
|---|---|---|
| **Photos from actual speakers/educators** | Real Brazilian people and their actual work | Yes, if the photographer's and subject's permissions cover this use |
| **[Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia)** | Files with item-level provenance, e.g. [Nilma Lino Gomes at the 2010 National Quilombola Education Seminar](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nilmalino.jpg) (Eliza Fiúza/ABr, CC BY 3.0 Brasil) | Depends on each file's licence; CC BY requires credit, licence link and noting changes. Use only in an accurately captioned educational context, never implying the person is a speaker or supporter |
| **[Nappy](https://nappy.co/license)** | Free photos focused on Black and Brown people (international) | Yes: CC0, commercial use and edits allowed. No implied endorsement; no degrading use. Not a Brazilian collection |
| **[Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/license/)** | Free photos and video | Yes, promotional use allowed; no implied endorsement. Not specialized; search labels don't prove ethnicity or nationality |
| **[Brasil com S](https://www.brasilcoms.com.br/termosdeuso)** (Lab 678) | Real Brazilian scenes and people | **No.** Free use is editorial only, excluding commercial and promotional use, and editing is prohibited. Contact them for custom production |

> [!NOTE]
> A generic "banco de imagens afro" could not be verified as a specific provider with a clear licence, and AFROPIXEL's terms for free authentic photography could not be confirmed. Do not use them until you have checked the current terms yourself. Old Agência Brasil licences also do not cover every current EBC image; check each file.

Keep a small record for every image: `file → creator → original URL → download date → who/what is shown → licence → allowed use → required credit → permitted edits → subject permission → reviewer`.

## Prompt patterns that work better

When an AI illustration is genuinely useful, these patterns reduce common failures. They are drafts, not guarantees. Two good outputs do not prove a model is fair. The Portuguese versions are on the [Portuguese page](../pt-BR/representation-bias.md).

**Lowest risk: typography and shapes, no people**

```prompt
Create a 15-second storyboard to promote CONAFROEDU 2026 to Brazilian teachers. Use readable typography and simple moving geometric shapes. Facts: 5 and 6 December 2026; 100% online; registration on Even3. Do not invent people, quotes, cultural symbols, images of previous editions or supporters. Leave certificate hours and conditions as placeholders to fill after the committee confirms them. Deliver the script, the screen order and a list of facts that need human review.
```

**Contemporary scene with specific roles, skin and hair**

```prompt
Contemporary editorial illustration, clearly not a documentary photograph. Three adult Black Brazilian teachers discuss a lesson plan on Afro-Brazilian history and culture in a present-day Brazilian school. A teacher with dark skin presents the plan, a teacher with brown skin organizes the references, and the third teacher asks questions and takes notes. Vary facial features and skin tones, with lighting that preserves detail in dark skin. Include short coily hair, braids and curly hair, drawn with care; these are the characters' choices, not a uniform for an identity. Everyday clothes, a plausible library and school materials, a computer used by the teachers themselves. Horizontal composition with space for text added later. No rescue scene, no poverty as visual shorthand, no white saviour, no generic "tribal" pattern, no exotic animals, no religious objects. Do not link the scene to real CONAFROEDU participants. Do not generate letters, names or logos.
```

Specific roles ("presents", "organizes") help keep authority with the people you intend. Vary roles across the campaign instead of repeating one hierarchy, and reject outputs that still clone faces or shift authority.

**Preserving a real person's identity (prefer a normal crop tool for this)**

```prompt
Use only the approved, authorized portrait of [name] for this layout. Preserve skin tone, facial features, hair texture and volume, age and personal marks. Adjust only framing and exposure, naturally; leave an area outside the photo for the name. Do not straighten hair, slim features, lighten skin or reconstruct the face. If the request would require changing identity, say so. The text will be added and checked separately.
```

**Culturally specific session: consult before drawing**

```prompt
We need to promote a session on [specific tradition/community, confirmed by the invited speaker]. Before suggesting any images, list what we should ask the responsible person: which terms they use, which symbols may appear publicly, which references they authorize and which elements must not be depicted. First propose a composition with the title, simple shapes and the authorized portrait. Do not invent orixás, rituals, graphic symbols, Adinkra meanings or a generic "African aesthetic". Separate what is confirmed from what depends on consultation.
```

**AI as a review helper (never as the approver)**

```prompt
Analyze this image as support for a human review: who is at the center, who teaches, who listens and who uses the technology? Are faces homogenized? Is skin tone associated with authority? Are there cultural elements without context, or a look of documentary evidence? List questions and possible problems. Do not infer the race or religion of real people and do not declare the image culturally correct. Approval is human.
```

> [!TIP]
> Do not just add "diverse" to a prompt. Research on video models found that explicit "debiasing" words can reduce some biases and worsen others. Describe concrete people, roles, context and what to avoid, then **check the output**.

## Religious and cultural symbols: handle with care

No controlled study has measured how accurately AI models depict orixás, Candomblé, Umbanda or Adinkra. Assume they get it wrong, and that a plausible-looking error is worse than no symbol.

- **Candomblé and Umbanda** are distinct religious traditions, not interchangeable labels or a standard set of images. A congress on Africanidades is not a religious congress, and being Black says nothing about a person's religion. Brazil's [national policy for traditional terreiro and African-matrix communities](https://www.gov.br/igualdaderacial/pt-br/assuntos/programas-e-projetos/politica-nacional-para-povos-e-comunidades-tradicionais-de-terreiro-e-matriz-africana/politica-nacional-para-povos-e-comunidades-tradicionais-de-terreiro-e-matriz-africana) emphasizes respect for their knowledge, practices and participation.
- **Orixás and sacred objects** must never be decorative backgrounds, mascots or sales imagery. If a specific session needs them, consult an appropriate practitioner about names, attributes, context and what must not be shown. One person cannot speak for every terreiro. Avoid anything that whitens, demonizes, sexualizes or turns sacred figures into fantasy characters. Do not generate them with AI.
- **Adinkra** is a symbolic language of Akan traditions in Ghana, not a set of generic "African icons" ([Ghana Ministry of Culture](https://www.motcca.gov.gh/traditional-symbols-in-ghana-adinkra/)). Verify each symbol's name, form and meaning; never let AI invent a glyph and assign it a meaning.
- **Kente** is Ghanaian strip-weaving knowledge, inscribed by [UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/craftsmanship-of-traditional-woven-textile-kente-02130) in 2024. Not every colourful zigzag is Kente, and it is not "Brazil's ancestral cloth".
- **Ubuntu** appears in CONAFROEDU's program. Ask the speaker to approve any explanation or quotation; do not use an AI one-liner as a summary of African philosophy.
- **Existing event graphics** use generalized African visual motifs. That is not permission to invent more symbols or meanings. A future visual audit can refine them, without making claims about the original designer's intent.
- **Black, African, Afro-Brazilian, Indigenous and quilombola** are not interchangeable identities. Lei 11.645/2008 covers Afro-Brazilian *and* Indigenous history; don't merge them into one image.

> [!TIP]
> When in doubt, use **typography and simple geometric shapes** with your brand colours. It is respectful, readable and fast. Rather than generating "something African", show the actual educational content.

## Avoiding poverty, white-saviour and exotic tropes

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Start from a real educational message and audience need | Start from "make something African" and accept the first result |
| Use consented photos of actual speakers | Fabricate a realistic audience or a "previous edition" |
| Show Black educators teaching, researching, leading, using technology | Show Black people only as students, recipients of help or in hardship |
| Show contemporary Brazilian settings: schools, libraries, universities, online sessions, urban and rural without hierarchy | Use savannahs, huts, animals or masks as a shortcut for "Africa" |
| Show joy, scholarship, debate and ordinary life | Make every image about suffering, slavery or rescue |
| Preserve skin tone, features and hair in permitted edits | Call lightening or straightening "professionalization" |
| Check roles and composition across the whole campaign grid | Treat one Black face at the edge of a group as inclusion |
| Use verified references for any specific symbol or textile | Mix Adinkra, Kente, orixás and invented scripts as decoration |
| Add Portuguese text in Canva after generating | Trust AI lettering for names, dates, laws, ISBN or DOI |
| Reject an output you cannot confidently review | Keep regenerating sacred imagery until it "looks right" |

For broader storytelling principles, the African-led organization Africa No Filter publishes guidance against white-saviour framing and for co-creation, consent and agency ([ethical storytelling](https://africanofilter.org/press-office/press-releases/new-handbook-outlines-how-to-write-about-africa-for-development-community/)). In Brazil, the [Rede JP/Peregum anti-racist communication manual](https://redejpcomunicacao.org/manual-antirracista/) is a professional reference on racial representation.

## Pre-publication review checklist

**Stop publication if any important item cannot be answered.** This checklist applies to every image *and* video, AI-generated or not.

**Facts and honesty**

- [ ] The message serves a real conference audience and matches the actual program.
- [ ] Every name, date, price, law number and call to action matches the approved fact sheet.
- [ ] Nothing implies a partnership, endorsement, previous edition or testimonial that doesn't exist.
- [ ] Real people's photos and voices have permission for this use; no unauthorized face or voice cloning.
- [ ] Any generated people are clearly illustrative, not presented as attendees or speakers.

**Representation**

- [ ] Black people appear with agency, varied expertise and appropriate prominence.
- [ ] Darker skin is not systematically linked to hardship or subordinate roles.
- [ ] Edits have not lightened skin, changed facial features or altered hair texture.
- [ ] Lighting preserves detail in dark skin without whitening it or hiding faces in shadow.
- [ ] Hair is varied and plausible (crespo, cacheado, tranças, locs, short, shaved, straightened by choice). No single style is "the" authentic or professional one.
- [ ] Faces, hands, bodies, devices, assistive technology and objects look credible; faces are not cloned.
- [ ] The setting is specific and appropriate; no savannah/hut/animal shorthand.
- [ ] No white-saviour or poverty default: Black educators explain, research, create and use technology.

**Culture and religion**

- [ ] No symbol, garment, script, word or religious object is used without a known reason and source.
- [ ] Sacred or culturally specific material was reviewed by relevant practitioners or knowledge-holders.
- [ ] Black, African, Afro-Brazilian, Indigenous and quilombola identities are not merged as equivalents.
- [ ] Historical interpretation is clearly distinguished from documentary evidence.

**Production**

- [ ] Videos were watched in full with sound: identity, skin, hair, voice, symbols and captions stay correct throughout.
- [ ] Portuguese names, accents, pronunciation and captions were checked by a person.
- [ ] Essential details also appear as text in the caption; alt text describes what is visible without guessing anyone's identity.
- [ ] Contrast, text size and mobile readability are adequate.
- [ ] AI use is disclosed and creative/photo credits are clear.
- [ ] Creator, source URL, licence, credit requirement and subject permission are recorded.
- [ ] A **named reviewer** with authority to request a replacement approved the final version.

**Practical review order:** (1) check caption and facts; (2) inspect the image at full size; (3) compare any portrait edit with the original side by side; (4) look at the **whole campaign grid** for patterns in skin tone and authority. Ask a Black Brazilian educator or designer, with agreed time and real decision power, to review representation, and a relevant practitioner for any religious tradition. If a concern can't be resolved, switch to an approved photo or to typography.

## Disclosure and honest labelling

- **Meta's rules:** Meta labels content it detects as AI-generated with "AI info" and **requires** you to use its disclosure tool for organic posts with photorealistic video or realistic audio that was created or altered digitally ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/)).
- **Your own label for illustrations:** *"Ilustração gerada com IA; não retrata participantes do evento."* ("AI-generated illustration; does not depict event participants.")
- **Disclosure does not fix a bad image.** The *Scientific Reports* study above found that stereotyped AI faces influenced viewers even when labelled as AI. Labelling is necessary; it is not enough.
- **Alt text:** describe what is visible ("Ilustração: três docentes conversam diante de um computador"), never guess a real person's race, religion or identity.

## Who decides

The most important safeguard is organizational, not technical. Give Black educators, designers and relevant knowledge-holders on or around the committee **meaningful decision power**: they must be able to reject an attractive but misleading image without having to justify it at length. Do not expect Black colleagues to do this review as unpaid, invisible extra work, and do not treat one person as speaking for every community. Write down who the reviewer is for each week of the [6-week sprint](conafroedu-sprint.md).
