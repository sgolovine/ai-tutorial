# CONAFROEDU 2026: conference context, Brazilian academic marketing, and responsible representation

**Research date:** 21 October 2026, America/Denver. **Campaign reference date:** 21 October 2026. **Scope:** evidence and recommendations for a small volunteer committee marketing a paid, national, online academic congress through Facebook-first organic communication. No outreach was sent and no accounts or event settings were changed.

## 1. Evidence, confidence, and the most important findings

Read first: `docs/BRIEF.md`, particularly “THE ACTUAL CONFERENCE,” and `docs/conference-announcement.md`. These establish the project brief and supplied promotional claims. Public research below supplements them; it does not certify the organizers, academic quality, affiliations, or eventual delivery of advertised benefits.

Evidence labels used throughout:

- **Verified public listing:** observed on a first-party event, institutional, government, publisher, or platform page on the research date. A published promise is evidence of what is advertised, not proof that it has already been delivered.
- **Supplied announcement:** supplied by the project, sometimes corroborated publicly.
- **Recommendation / inference:** an editorial or operational proposal derived from the evidence, not a measured result or an organizer decision.
- **[UNVERIFIED]:** missing, conflicting, inaccessible, or not independently established. Do not silently convert these items into public-facing facts.

### Immediate implications for the website

1. **The submission campaign has only two weeks left.** The live Even3 submission section and two organizer graphics give **4 November 2026** as the deadline. The congress itself is **5–6 December**. A generic six-week campaign that keeps asking for papers through late November would be wrong. [Event](https://www.even3.com.br/iconafroedu-764690/), [submission graphic](https://static.even3.com/pagina-evento/1001001484.dada13e93fdc4ba7b83a.png), [calendar graphic](https://static.even3.com/pagina-evento/1001001426.093a8a526e0446bea557.png).
2. **20 November should support attendance and pedagogical engagement.** It falls after the currently published paper deadline. Connect Novembro Negro to year-round anti-racist practice, with a registration CTA appropriate to the actual phase.
3. **Use displayed ticket prices, not search metadata.** The rendered ticket page shows prices different from the lower values in its structured metadata. The browser-rendered prices are recorded below; the final checkout total remains [UNVERIFIED].
4. **Publication has an additional advertised charge.** The event separately lists **R$ 16,98 per work** for proceedings publication. Do not imply that a ticket mentioning one submission necessarily includes that charge.
5. **Presentation requirements conflict.** Rules and slide instructions say presentations are optional; the FAQ says they are mandatory. The committee should resolve this before AI writes submission FAQs or automated responses.
6. **AI representation is part of the event’s credibility.** Real participant photography with permission, credited Black Brazilian creative work, and clear typography are strong defaults. Generated people, cultural symbols, religious imagery, and historical scenes require human review; adding “diverse” to a prompt is insufficient.

### 3.1 Cost details worth teaching accurately

The [official marketing features page](https://plataforma.even3.com.br/solucoes/divulgacao-de-eventos/) advertises no extra charge for its marketing tools, free use for free registrations, and a **10% charge on paid registrations**. Search excerpts still showed **R$ 12,90**; the live article/API updated on 21 October 2026 shows R$ 19,90. **Do not apply the free-event charge to CONAFROEDU**, which lists paid tickets, or announce a separate certificate fee without an event-specific basis.

### 4.3 Academic value propositions, with honest qualifications

| Audience / value | Useful framing | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Basic-education teachers | Exchange classroom practices, curriculum approaches, and ERER implementation experiences | “Elimine o racismo da sua escola em dois dias” or promises that the event satisfies all professional training obligations. |
| Undergraduate students | Participation, research initiation, exchange with educators; advertised certificate workload | Guaranteed acceptance as **horas complementares**. Acceptance depends on the course’s rules. |
| Postgraduate students and researchers | Present and discuss ongoing/completed work; proceedings, accurate bibliographic records, thematic networking | Guaranteed selection-process points, journal equivalence, or automatic PPG recognition. |
| School managers | Reflection on school response, curriculum, teacher development, and community participation | Promising a PNEERQ credential, accreditation, or MEC approval without documented authorization. |
| Community educators / social movements | Recognition of situated practices, collective knowledge, territory, and educational experience | Framing community knowledge as raw material for outsiders or implying academic credentials are the only valid expertise. |
| Publication | ISBN identifies a publication; DOI supports persistent identification and discovery | “ISBN/DOI garante qualidade”, automatic indexation, a journal impact factor, or guaranteed citations. |

**Qualis needs a 2026 update.** ISBN/DOI does not establish a Qualis rating. CAPES announced on **23 May 2025** that the **2025–2028** evaluation shifts toward classification of articles, with procedures selected/combined by evaluation area. Historical Qualis data may still matter in particular institutional rules, but the guide should not market proceedings as automatically “Qualis A” or promise points. Verify the relevant area, current notice, and actual publication vehicle. [CAPES announcement](https://www.gov.br/capes/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/avaliacao-da-producao-intelectual-e-ampliada).

Similarly, professional progression depends on employer/career rules and the participant’s circumstances. “Certificado de 40 horas” is an advertised event offer; “garantia de progressão funcional” is not.

### 4.4 Deadline extension: recognizable convention, not a script to fake

**Observed:** COPENE’s 2026 bulletin extended full-work delivery to **31 July 2026** and specified the participant area as the submission route. This is evidence that **prorrogação de prazo** is a recognizable academic communication format, not proof that every event should extend deadlines. **Recommendations:**

- Announce an extension only after the scientific committee decides it and the live platform is updated.

- Show the previous and new dates, the cutoff time/timezone if confirmed, and any downstream effects.
- Preserve fair treatment for people who complied with the earlier deadline.
- Avoid a preplanned series of false “última chance” claims, invented demand, “a pedidos” without evidence, or fake limited capacity for an online event.
- Correct scheduled posts, the pinned Facebook invitation, WhatsApp forwardable text, email templates, graphics, and FAQs together.

### 8.3 Specific representational failure modes to inspect

| Risk | What can go wrong | Practical response |
|---|---|---|
| Colorism | Light-skinned people dominate prestigious roles while dark-skinned people appear mainly as needy students or in hardship scenes. | Review the whole campaign together: who teaches, leads, speaks, researches, and receives care? Include dark-skinned Black people in varied authoritative and ordinary roles. |
| Hair and “beautification” | Straightening, loosening curls, replacing protective styles, changing hairlines, or making dark skin lighter during portrait enhancement. | Prefer original approved photos. Compare edits side by side; specify preservation of identity and texture, then inspect the result. |
| Homogenized faces | Many supposedly different people share the same face, features, age, body type, or styling. | Review variation, age, gender, disability, and regional context without forcing a demographic checklist into every single image. |
| Generic Africa | Savannahs, animals, huts, masks, and invented “tribal” clothing used for a contemporary Brazilian education event. | Specify a real place/context and the educational activity; include contemporary classrooms, research, books, digital participation, urban and rural life without hierarchy. |
| Cultural collage | Unrelated cloth, scripts, masks, ornaments, and religious objects combined as an “African aesthetic.” | Identify the actual culture and source for each element. If no real reason exists to use a symbol, use typography and ordinary geometric composition. |
| Erasure of knowledge | Black people pictured as recipients while white people appear as experts or controllers of technology. | Check spatial position, size, gaze, action, microphone ownership, and who explains the lesson. |
| Poverty or trauma default | Every Black/African educational scene becomes a story of deprivation, slavery, suffering, or rescue. | Include scholarship, creativity, collective agency, joy, work, disagreement, and ordinary life. Do not erase historical violence, but use it only when substantively relevant. |
| False documentation | Synthetic classrooms, audiences, prior editions, testimonials, or speakers look like real evidence. | Never use invented participation as social proof. Mark illustration and AI use clearly; use actual, permitted documentation for factual claims. |
| Video drift | Skin shade, facial identity, hair, clothing, symbols, or accent changes between frames; a “neutral” voice becomes caricature. | Review the full video with sound, including opening/closing frames and captions; use consented speaker audio or text-based motion graphics. |
| Historical invention | AI invents a historical figure’s portrait, quote, biography, event, or garment and presents it as an archive. | Use verified sources and genuine archival material with appropriate rights. Label interpretive illustration; do not manufacture quotations. |

These are editorial review categories grounded in the research, not measured frequencies for all tools. Never use a model or automated classifier to “verify” the race, religion, or quilombola identity of real people from appearance.

### 8.4 Candomblé, Umbanda, orixás, Adinkra, Kente, and Ubuntu

**Evidence gap:** this scan did not establish a robust 2023–2026 controlled benchmark specifically measuring generator accuracy for Afro-Brazilian orixás, Candomblé, Umbanda, and Adinkra. The safeguards below combine cultural-source evidence with editorial judgment. Do not invent a study or a numerical error rate for these traditions.

- **Religious diversity:** Candomblé and Umbanda are not interchangeable labels or a single standardized iconography. A congress on Africanidades is not necessarily a religious congress, and Black identity does not establish an individual’s religion. The Brazilian policy for traditional terreiro and African-matrix communities emphasizes respect for their cultures, knowledge, practices, and participation. [MIR policy](https://www.gov.br/igualdaderacial/pt-br/assuntos/programas-e-projetos/politica-nacional-para-povos-e-comunidades-tradicionais-de-terreiro-e-matriz-africana/politica-nacional-para-povos-e-comunidades-tradicionais-de-terreiro-e-matriz-africana).
- **Sacred imagery:** do not use orixás, ritual clothing, sacred objects, or invented ceremonies as generic decorative backgrounds. If a specific session requires them, consult an appropriate practitioner/knowledge-holder about names, attributes, context, permissibility, and what must not be depicted. One reviewer cannot speak for every terreiro or tradition. Avoid whitewashing, demonizing, sexualizing, or conflating sacred figures with fantasy characters.
- **Adinkra:** Ghana’s cultural sources describe a meaningful symbolic language associated with Akan traditions; it is not a collection of arbitrary universal “African icons.” Verify the symbol’s name, form, context, and interpretation through knowledgeable sources. Do not ask AI to invent a glyph and assign it a scholarly meaning. [Ghana Ministry of Tourism, Culture & Creative Arts](https://www.motcca.gov.gh/traditional-symbols-in-ghana-adinkra/), [National Commission on Culture](https://ghanaculture.gov.gh/world-recognition-of-adinkra/).
- **Kente:** UNESCO describes Ghanaian strip-weaving knowledge, skills, and social context, inscribed in **2024**. Do not label any colorful zigzag a Kente pattern or represent it as Brazil’s single ancestral cloth. Avoid assuming that heritage is permission to copy a contemporary artist’s design. [UNESCO listing](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/craftsmanship-of-traditional-woven-textile-kente-02130).
- **Ubuntu:** a named CONAFROEDU program item makes it relevant, but do not use an AI-generated one-line slogan as a complete account of African philosophy, or imply it is a concept shared identically across all African cultures. Ask the actual speaker to approve an explanation and quotation.
- **Current branding:** existing event graphics include generalized African visual motifs. Their presence is not blanket authorization to invent further symbols, traditions, or cultural explanations. A visual audit can refine future assets without making unsupported claims about the original artist’s intent.

For broader storytelling practice, [Africa No Filter’s ethical storytelling resources](https://africanofilter.org/what-we-do/advocacy/) are an African-led reference: use specificity, agency, context, and multiplicity rather than a continent-wide deficit narrative. Its handbook is [openly linked here](https://africanofilter.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/How-to-tell-an-african-story.pdf). The upload directory is not necessarily the handbook’s publication date.

## 9. Brazilian academic-audience copy conventions

### 9.1 Tone and vocabulary

**Recommendation:** academic but welcoming, community-minded, specific, and accessible. Address teachers as professionals with experience to share. Explain a technical term at first use. Avoid literal English marketing translations, inflated credentials, and an urgency tone that overwhelms the educational purpose.

| Term / convention | Appropriate use | Important distinction |
|---|---|---|
| **Chamada de trabalhos** / **chamada para submissão de trabalhos** | Formal invitation stating scope, modalities, dates, rules, and review | An **edital** suggests a formal document; do not call a social caption an edital if no such document exists. |
| **Inscrição** | A person’s event registration | Different from submitting a work or paying a publication fee. |
| **Submissão** | Sending a work for evaluation | Does not mean acceptance, presentation, or publication. |
| **Parecer**, **avaliação**, **carta de aceite** | Scientific review and its documented result | Do not describe AI-generated praise as a scientific review. |
| **Eixos / áreas temáticas** | Organize submissions by actual event categories | Copy the live category name accurately; do not invent a thematic track. |
| **Artigo completo**, **resumo expandido**, **resumo simples** | Distinct submission formats | Page limits and structure depend on this event’s rules. |
| **Relato de experiência / práticas exitosas** | Reflective account of educational practice | Do not imply that effectiveness has been independently proven or that consent/ethics can be skipped. |
| **Anais** | Proceedings collecting event work | Not automatically a journal, book chapter, or Qualis-rated publication. |
| **Ouvinte / participante**, **autor(a)**, **apresentador(a)** | Different participation roles | Different roles may have different requirements/certificates. |
| **Carga horária**, **certificado de participação** | State documented hours and conditions | Not a diploma, professional license, MEC recognition, or guaranteed credit. |
| **Prorrogação de prazo**, **virada de lote** | Real changes to specific deadlines or prices | Keep scientific and commercial milestones separate. |
| **ERER** | Educação para as Relações Étnico-Raciais, explained at first mention | Do not expect an unfamiliar teacher to know every policy acronym. |
| **Pessoas negras**, **população negra**, **culturas afro-brasileiras** | Use contextual, respectful wording and self-identification | Avoid euphemisms such as “moreno” to label someone, or treating “negro,” “africano,” and “quilombola” as synonyms. |

Brazilian calls often use **pesquisadoras e pesquisadores**, **professoras e professores**, or an institution’s established inclusive style. Use a consistent readable form and accessible sentence structure. Avoid inventing a universal required gender style or assuming every audience wants the same form.

Conventions that improve clarity:

- Write **4 de novembro de 2026** in prose; use **04/11/2026** in compact calendars. In English, write **4 November 2026** to avoid month/day ambiguity.
- Give **horário de Brasília (UTC−3)** when the committee confirms the exact cutoff/session time. Do not use the researcher’s America/Denver timezone in participant-facing copy.
- Format money as **R$ 55,00** and hours as **40 horas**. Avoid “40h de aulas ao vivo” unless that duration is actually documented.
- Put the main action first: submit a work **or** register as a participant. Link to the relevant instructions and a single authoritative registration page.
- Use the event’s full name once, then CONAFROEDU; preserve Portuguese names and diacritics.
- Few purposeful emojis can work in social posts. A formal list/email call should remain readable without relying on icons or all caps.
- Limit hashtags to relevant discoverability; supplied tags are starting points, not proven growth tactics.
