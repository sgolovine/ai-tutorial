# Organic Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp marketing for an education conference in Brazil

**Research date:** 2026-10-21. **Project:** `docs/BRIEF.md`. **Audience:** a small, non-specialist team; organic Facebook first; no advertising budget. Main analysis is in English, with original Brazilian Portuguese examples for adaptation into the bilingual guide.

## 1. How to read this research

This document distinguishes three kinds of material:

- **Documented findings:** platform statements, product documentation and research results, linked to numbered sources. Publication dates, measurement periods and access limitations appear in **SOURCES**. All sources were checked on **2026-10-21**.
- **Recommended experiments:** this researcher's proposed operating plan, sample schedules, checklists and prompts. These are practical starting points, not measured guarantees or rules imposed by Meta.
- **[UNVERIFIED]:** a claim whose present applicability, regional rollout, account eligibility or underlying evidence could not be established. Do not turn these into categorical statements in the website.

Several Facebook Help Center and Facebook for Creators URLs redirected to login or returned temporary blocking messages. Where their indexed article text was available, that text was read; the source register identifies this limitation. Meta Newsroom articles were readable. The Transparency Center Feed system-card endpoint returned HTTP 429, so the ranking explanation also uses Meta's own AI and engineering publications. No authenticated conference account was inspected. This is research, not a confirmation of the features available to the team's Page.

### Decisions that follow from the evidence

1. **Publish useful, original education content featuring real people.** Make the conference the place to continue a worthwhile conversation, rather than making every post a poster asking for registration. Meta's 2026 originality guidance and 2025 anti-spam changes support this direction. [S10–S11]
2. **Give each channel a job.** Use Facebook Page content and Events as the public home; Groups for relevant discussion; Instagram for speaker collaborations and discovery; WhatsApp for voluntary updates and questions. This is a recommended allocation, informed by Brazilian audience evidence, not a claim about universal conversion performance. [S01–S05]
3. **Measure completed registrations and attendance.** Views, followers and Facebook event responses are intermediate signals. Ticket-platform data and tagged links make an organic campaign accountable. [S24, S41–S44]
4. **Test timing and formats locally.** Published global studies disagree on posting times and measure different outcomes. None establishes the best schedule for Brazilian teachers. [S16–S20]
5. **Use AI to draft, adapt and organize; have people verify and approve.** Start from approved facts and real expertise. Do not invent speaker endorsements, testimonials, prices, credentials, certificates or classroom results.

## 2. Brazil: Facebook remains useful, but the audience crosses Meta apps

### 2.1 What the available numbers actually mean

DataReportal's **Digital 2026: Brazil** was published in November 2025 and primarily describes October 2025. Its figures are not an October 2026 census. The following platform figures describe **potential advertising audiences**, useful for orientation even when buying no ads; they are not deduplicated monthly active users. [S01]

| Indicator | Reported figure | Implication for this project |
|---|---:|---|
| Internet users | 185 million | Design for mobile access, but offer institutional/offline routes too |
| Social-media user identities | 150 million | Identities are not necessarily unique people |
| Facebook potential ad audience | 109 million | Facebook still has substantial Brazilian scale |
| Instagram potential ad audience | 147 million | Instagram deserves a distribution role alongside Facebook |
| Facebook adult ad audience, female share | 53.9% | A national aggregate, not a profile of this conference |

Do not add Facebook and Instagram audiences together: people overlap. Do not describe a change in advertising estimates as proven user migration. [S01]

WhatsApp is particularly important. Mobile Time, discussing its own 2026 Super Panorama research, reports installation on more than 98% of Brazilian smartphones. The survey landing page specifies **4,138 smartphone owners**, fieldwork **4–24 March 2026**, and a reported **1.5 percentage-point margin of error**. Installation is not daily usage, notification attention or permission to contact somebody. The full 2026 report required a registration form and was not downloaded; the installation statistic was checked in the publisher's public reporting. [S02–S03]

RD Station's 2026 research supplies useful business context: its introduction distinguishes an online questionnaire from operational data generated by RD products in 2025. The methodology states **2,790 respondents**, despite the rounded “3,000” description elsewhere. In its customer-service chapter, WhatsApp accounts for **97% of messages recorded in RD Station Conversas**. This is a vendor-customer dataset, not WhatsApp's share of all Brazilian communication. The same chapter reports measurement difficulties and manual workload among surveyed marketers, which supports keeping this team's workflow simple. [S04–S05]

### 2.2 Who is still on Facebook?

Avoid “Facebook is only for older people.” NapoleonCat's September 2026 addressable-audience estimates place **25–34** first, at **29.2%**, followed by **35–44**, at **20.9%**; they also show substantial older groups. However, its Facebook total of about **182.7 million** differs sharply from DataReportal's 109 million for late 2025, and some demographic figures differ within NapoleonCat's own page. **[UNVERIFIED] precise current Brazilian Facebook population and demographic shares.** Do not merge these datasets or use them to calculate growth. The age pattern is a tentative planning clue, not a verified teacher profile. [S06]

**[UNVERIFIED] No strong, current source located establishes what proportion of Brazilian teachers, professors, school leaders or university students actively uses Facebook.** National platform estimates do not identify occupations. An education conference may have an excellent Facebook niche even if a younger segment prefers Instagram, but that must be tested.

Recommended audience research before production:

- Ask five teachers, three school coordinators and three university students where they actually discover professional events.
- Ask existing speakers which channels their educator communities use.
- Inspect the Page's own cities, age ranges and engagement, where Insights provides enough data.
- Review relevant public groups manually: recent questions, moderator rules, actual conversations and geography matter more than member totals.
- Add an optional registration question: “Como você ficou sabendo do evento?” Include multiple channels and free text.

### 2.3 Channel roles for a small team

| Surface | Recommended job | Education-sector example | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facebook Page | Public identity, practical content, trust | Teacher explains one classroom problem the conference addresses | Relevant reach, conversations, registration visits |
| Facebook Event | Stable event listing and updates | Dates, city, programme, accessibility and ticket destination | Event interest followed by actual registrations |
| Existing Facebook Groups | Participate in an established community | Answer a methodology question, then share a permitted relevant session | Useful discussion and qualified visits |
| Own Facebook Group | Ongoing peer learning, if moderators have capacity | Monthly exchange of teaching practices before and after the event | Member-to-member conversations |
| Instagram | Speaker collaboration and visual discovery | Joint speaker Reel, practical carousel, Stories questions | Non-follower reach, profile visits, registrations |
| WhatsApp Channel | Opt-in public updates | Programme updates and real deadline reminders | Update views and tagged-link registrations |
| WhatsApp Community | Organize related participant groups | Regional travel groups or thematic peer groups | Useful coordination and fewer repeated support questions |
| WhatsApp Business conversation | Answer individual questions | Payment, accessibility, certificates, arrival instructions | Resolved questions and completed registration |
| WhatsApp Status | Voluntary sharing by the team and ambassadors | A short personal invitation | Replies and tagged visits |

This allocation is a recommendation. It does not imply that every surface should be launched immediately. A Page, one Event, a connected Instagram account and one WhatsApp update destination can be enough.

## 3. Facebook Feed and recommendations in 2026

### 3.1 There is no single public “algorithm formula”

Meta describes multiple systems for Feed, recommendations, Reels, Stories, comments and Groups. Its system-card explanation names signals such as prior interaction with an author, reactions/comments/views, and viewing duration. Its engineering description explains candidate inventory, predictions and ranking rather than a fixed public points table. [S07–S08]

A useful simplified model is:

1. **Find eligible candidates:** content from connections and content that might be recommended.
2. **Evaluate signals:** the viewer's behavior, the author relationship, the content and its reception.
3. **Predict value:** estimate whether that particular viewer will find it useful or interact with it.
4. **Rank and apply quality/integrity rules:** assemble the experience, with feedback changing subsequent recommendations.

This is an explanatory simplification of Meta's publications, not a reconstruction of its current production code. **[UNVERIFIED] Exact weights, universal engagement thresholds, a required first-hour engagement target, and any fixed organic reach percentage.** [S07–S08]

Recommendation eligibility is a separate issue from whether content is allowed to stay online. Meta publishes additional Recommendation Guidelines governing content it recommends to people who do not follow the account. Being compliant does not guarantee recommendations or reach. [S09]

### 3.2 Recent changes that matter

| Announcement | Documented change | Recommended response |
|---|---|---|
| April 2025: spam reduction | Accounts using distracting, excessive hashtags or unrelated captions can lose distribution to non-followers; coordinated fake engagement is targeted | Keep captions related to the actual material; moderate spam; avoid engagement schemes [S11] |
| June 2025: video publishing | Meta announced that new Facebook videos would move into Reels, without the former length/format restrictions | Avoid teaching the old 90-second Facebook maximum as a current universal rule; check the actual composer [S12] |
| October 2025: Reels recommendations | Meta described faster interest learning, fresher recommendations and feedback from saves/“Not Interested” | Use timely, specific education topics and clear openings; inspect retention [S13] |
| December 2025: interface changes | Multi-photo Feed posts move to a standardized grid; creation and cross-posting controls change | Preview Facebook multi-image posts independently; do not assume Instagram's exact carousel presentation [S14] |
| March 2026: originality | Creator-produced content and substantive transformation are favored over copies and minimal alterations | Record speakers and organizers, demonstrate real practices, retain source files and permissions [S10] |
| October 13, 2026: “Your algorithm” | Meta announced topic controls and natural-language feedback for Feed/Reels, with recommendations adapting within the session | Make subject matter recognizable; do not infer a new keyword-stuffing trick. **[UNVERIFIED] Brazil/account availability** [S15] |

Meta's October 2025 announcement also says videos longer than one minute represented over half of Facebook watch time in the described creator context. That is evidence against “shorter is always better,” not a prescription to make every conference Reel long. The company did not establish a Brazil education benchmark. [S13]

### 3.3 What “original” means for a conference

Meta's March 2026 guidance treats content produced directly by a Page/profile owner as original. Third-party clips can qualify when the creator adds substantial new information, analysis or storytelling. Minimal changes, passive reactions and simple narration of existing footage can be deprioritized; repeatedly publishing predominantly unoriginal content can affect account recommendability. Permission and creative originality are distinct questions. [S10]

Recommended assets:

- A teacher demonstrates a classroom activity and explains one decision behind it.
- A confirmed speaker answers a real question collected from educators.
- Organizers explain the programme choices or show the actual venue.
- Participants share their own takeaways, with permission for reuse and attribution.
- An authorized talk excerpt is introduced with a meaningful lesson and edited for the intended audience.

Avoid building the campaign from downloaded viral videos, lightly altered memes, stock footage with unrelated educational captions, or fabricated AI crowds. Cross-posting a video the team actually produced is different from copying somebody else's work; still verify music, speaker rights and each platform's settings.

### 3.4 Translate engagement signals into useful content

| Practical objective | Useful creative choice | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Help somebody understand the topic quickly | Show the classroom question in the first scene and caption | A long animated logo before any information |
| Hold attention | Deliver a concrete example before the event invitation | A suspense hook that never pays off |
| Encourage meaningful replies | Ask about an actual teaching constraint, and answer responses | “Comente SIM para provar que valoriza a educação” |
| Make sharing worthwhile | Provide a checklist colleagues can use | Requiring shares/tags to access basic event facts |
| Build author familiarity | Use recurring real speakers and a consistent editorial voice | Anonymous, generic AI text every day |
| Reduce negative feedback | Match headline, image, topic and destination | Misleading urgency, unrelated captions or repeated promotion |

These are recommended creative applications. Meta has not published a guaranteed conversion between a save, comment or watch-time increment and extra distribution. [S07, S11, S49]

### 3.5 Links: preserve the registration path

Buffer's 2026 report finds Facebook format gaps relatively small: median engagement rates of **5.20% for images**, **4.84% for video**, **4.76% for text**, and **4.43% for links**, within its dataset. These rates are not reach percentages or ticket conversion rates. Its methodology uses reactions, comments and shares for Facebook engagement and generally divides by reach. The report analyzes Buffer-published content, mainly 2025, rather than all Facebook posts. [S16]

This does **not** prove Facebook applies the same penalty to every external URL. Nor does the predominance of non-link views in a US content report establish that putting a link in the first comment improves Brazilian event sales. **[UNVERIFIED] A universal “link in comments” organic advantage.**

There is a separate product issue: December 2025 reporting described a limited test restricting some non-subscribing professional accounts/Pages to two external-link posts monthly. Current Brazil coverage and exact rules could not be established. **[UNVERIFIED] A universal two-link monthly rule, whether comments count, and whether this conference's account is affected.** [S36]

Meta's September 2026 Meta One announcement now describes optional paid professional features, including links in organic posts/Reels and enhanced analytics, while stating the core app experience stays free. Benefits vary by app, account and region. This is not evidence that ordinary website links require a subscription everywhere. [S35]

Recommended approach:

- Use a clear ticket/registration URL where the account permits it; test on a phone.
- Keep the registration destination in the Event details and the Page's available website/action area.
- Use Instagram's available profile/Story link surfaces; do not assume caption URLs become clickable.
- Keep useful posts useful without requiring an external click; include direct links on posts whose purpose is registration.
- If comparing an in-post link with a comment link, compare completed registrations and user friction, not just reach.
- If an account displays a link restriction, record its exact notice and adapt around permitted surfaces. Do not buy verification by default, or evade limits with spam accounts or obfuscated URLs.

## 4. Facebook Events: a public information hub

### 4.1 Set up the canonical event

Meta's Help Center states that **events hosted by a Page are public**, and the privacy setting cannot be changed after creation. Event posting controls can include host-only posting or approval. [S21]

Recommended setup checklist; labels and optional fields must be checked in the live interface:

- [ ] Switch to the actual organizing Page and confirm appropriate access. Meta identifies event creation/editing among Facebook-access capabilities; do not assume task access exposes identical controls. [S23]
- [ ] Create one canonical Event for the edition, with a recognizable name and city or online format.
- [ ] Enter start **and end** dates, times and the correct event time zone.
- [ ] State venue, address, online arrangements and transport information when confirmed.
- [ ] Identify the intended audience: for example, professores da educação básica, licenciandos, gestores or pesquisadores.
- [ ] Describe concrete learning opportunities. Include only verified speakers and sessions.
- [ ] State price/lot, registration destination, refund information and certificate conditions accurately.
- [ ] Add an available ticket-link field; also make registration instructions clear in the description.
- [ ] Include accessibility information and a contact for accommodation questions.
- [ ] Use a readable cover and repeat essential information in text.
- [ ] Choose discussion moderation settings and assign one responder.
- [ ] Preview as an ordinary visitor on mobile, follow the ticket link and check dates.

The optional ticket-URL/co-host model is documented in the NCSL Facebook guide, which points to Meta's Page-event help article; Meta's direct article was blocked during research. **[UNVERIFIED] Exact October 2026 creation-menu path and which optional fields appear for this Page.** Prefer the concepts above over a brittle screenshot tutorial. [S25]

### 4.2 Co-hosts and partners

Invite a real venue, university or organizing partner as co-host where supported. Get the partner's agreement and confirm any acceptance flow. Co-hosting is a management relationship, not simply a logo placement; agree who can edit dates, programme and description. Do not add an institution merely because a professor works there.

Eventbrite's official Facebook integration guide documents Page-based publication and co-host configuration, subject to Page administration and event eligibility. **[UNVERIFIED] The exact same integration is available to every Brazilian organizer.** A direct link to the ticket page remains the fallback. [S26]

### 4.3 Keep the Event alive without flooding it

Recommended discussion rhythm:

- Launch: a welcome post with programme, registration and FAQ destinations.
- Weekly: one meaningful speaker question, practical takeaway or programme update.
- Deadline periods: one advance reminder, then a genuinely useful final reminder with exact date/time.
- Final week: arrival, accessibility, what to bring and where support is available.
- Event day: actual schedule changes and useful navigation information.
- Afterwards: resources, feedback and the recap.

Use clear prompts such as “Qual dúvida você gostaria que a mesa sobre avaliação discutisse?” Have a person acknowledge answers. Do not tag the entire audience routinely or turn every discussion thread into another sales poster.

### 4.4 Responses and reminders are not registration

Facebook distinguishes event responses such as **Going** and **Interested**, with visibility controls. These are not ticket orders. [S24]

**[UNVERIFIED] A guaranteed reminder schedule or notification delivery to every interested person.** Notifications depend on settings and product behavior. Offer a voluntary WhatsApp update destination and a calendar invitation from the registration process when supported. Never scrape the interested/going list for email or WhatsApp outreach.

## 5. Groups: participate first, create only if useful

### 5.1 Existing Brazilian education communities

Recommended manual search terms:

| Audience | Search examples | Match the contribution to |
|---|---|---|
| Basic-education teachers | `professores [cidade/estado]`, `educação infantil [estado]`, `alfabetização professores` | Classroom realities, travel feasibility, school calendar |
| Subject teachers | `professores de matemática`, `ensino de ciências`, `educação inclusiva` | A relevant session or useful teaching example |
| University students | `pedagogia [universidade]`, `licenciatura [instituição]`, `centro acadêmico` | Student pricing, participation opportunities, programme relevance |
| Researchers/professors | `pesquisa em educação`, `pós-graduação educação [região]` | Call for papers, methods, peer exchange |
| School management | `coordenação pedagógica`, `gestores escolares [estado]` | Team development, timetable and institutional participation |

**[UNVERIFIED] Membership, quality and posting permissions of any group found using these searches.** No specific group is endorsed here, and search availability is not permission to promote.

Participation sequence:

1. Read the rules, recent posts and pinned announcements.
2. Identify yourself honestly as part of the organizing team.
3. Ask moderators for permission when event promotion is restricted or unclear.
4. Contribute an answer, resource or discussion suited to that specific group.
5. If allowed, explain why a particular event session is relevant and disclose your connection.
6. Return to answer questions. Stop if moderators decline.

Suggested permission request, to send manually only after the team approves outreach:

> Olá, [nome]. Faço parte da organização do [evento], em [cidade/data]. Vi que o grupo conversa bastante sobre [tema específico]. Podemos compartilhar uma publicação com [recurso útil] e as informações da mesa sobre esse tema? Se houver um formato ou dia próprio para divulgação, seguimos a orientação de vocês.

Do not copy this unchanged into dozens of groups. Do not use fake accounts, automated joining, scraped member lists, unsolicited mass DMs or repeated identical comment links.

### 5.2 The team's own group

An annual event alone may not justify a permanent group. Recommended launch criterion: there is a continuing learning purpose, a named moderator and enough participants prepared to ask and answer questions.

Possible purpose: “Troca de práticas e formação continuada — comunidade do [evento].” Keep a pinned welcome, conduct rules, disclosure rules, a weekly discussion and a resource index. Set boundaries against identifiable student information and unapproved promotional posts. Tell members what happens after the conference.

Meta documents group-event creation and notes that posting options and approval requirements depend on admin settings; private and public groups also have different invitation/visibility behavior. Group membership does not promise delivery of every post. [S22]

If the team cannot support moderation, concentrate on the public Event and partner communities instead of launching an unattended group.

## 6. Formats and cross-platform production

### 6.1 Choose a format by the job

The following lengths and quantities are **production experiments**, not Meta limits or Brazil benchmarks.

| Format | Recommended first experiment | Example | Primary metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original Reel | 20–45 seconds, one question and answer | “O que fazer quando a turma copia uma resposta de IA sem compreender?” | Retention, non-follower reach, relevant visits |
| Longer native video/Reel | 2–5 minutes when explanation requires it | Speaker works through a real teaching decision | Average watch time, useful questions |
| Real photo + caption | One person, one story, one useful point | Teacher explains why the session matters to their practice | Meaningful comments, shares, registrations |
| Multi-image post/carousel | 4–6 readable panels | Problem → example → options → session invitation | Shares/saves where available, visits |
| Plain text | One focused discussion | “Qual etapa do planejamento mais toma tempo hoje?” | Substantive replies |
| Story | A short sequence of 2–4 frames | Question, speaker answer, next action | Replies, exits, link actions where available |
| Live | A rehearsed 15–25-minute conversation | Two speakers answer pre-submitted questions | Relevant attendance, retention, registrations |
| Direct link post | Accurate benefit and deadline | Registration opens or a real price lot closes | Completed registration per visit |

Metricool's 2026 study examines approximately **39.8 million organic posts** across **1.06 million connected accounts**, including posts published natively. It reports Facebook average reach up **51%**, impressions **57%** and interactions **56%** against 2024. These are sample-level observations about 2025 performance, not a promise for a new Page. The study and Buffer's findings justify testing several formats rather than assuming Facebook is no longer useful. [S16–S17]

### 6.2 Reels production checklist

- [ ] Record a real speaker/teacher with permission and a clean master file.
- [ ] Start with the educator's problem, not the event logo.
- [ ] Give one useful example before the promotional CTA.
- [ ] Use clear speech, readable captions and adequate light.
- [ ] Keep important text clear of interface overlays; preview in each app.
- [ ] Check the exact name, role, date, city and registration instructions.
- [ ] Use rights-cleared audio. Do not assume a music license transfers across apps or to external promotion.
- [ ] Upload the clean original or use supported cross-posting; do not download a watermarked repost as the production master.
- [ ] Check public visibility and the receiving Facebook identity.
- [ ] Save the original and final captioned versions for future reuse.

Meta documents separate Facebook Reel audience settings, including for Reels shared from Instagram; the audiences are not necessarily identical. Cross-posting is a publishing convenience, not a guarantee of identical reach or permissions. [S30]

### 6.3 Carousels, collaborative posts and cross-posting

An Instagram carousel is useful for a sequence such as “Três perguntas para avaliar uma ferramenta de IA.” On Facebook, the same images may appear as a grid or multi-photo post, so each image should make sense independently and the main caption should carry the argument. Meta's December 2025 interface announcement supports this distinction. [S14]

Instagram officially supports **Collabs** for co-authored posts, carousels and Reels; the invited account accepts, and the post can appear on participating profiles. Its 2023 announcement described up to three invited collaborators. **[UNVERIFIED] That historic cap is still the current maximum.** Use the actual composer to check. [S29]

Recommended collaboration: the conference and a confirmed speaker jointly publish one genuinely useful explanation. Ask the speaker to adapt the opening to their community and respond to relevant comments. Do not describe this as guaranteed delivery to every follower.

**[UNVERIFIED] Current universal availability and exact behavior of Facebook co-authored/collaborator posts for Brazilian Pages, and whether an Instagram collaboration preserves all collaborator behavior when cross-posted.** Distinguish Instagram Collabs from Facebook Event co-hosts and a normal tag/share. The fallback is a speaker-authored post, approved assets, proper attribution and a unique tracked registration link.

### 6.4 Stories and Lives

Recommended Stories themes: behind-the-scenes preparation, a speaker's personal invitation, an honest deadline, a short FAQ, the venue route, and live schedule updates. Add questions, countdowns or links only where the specific account supports them. **[UNVERIFIED] Identical stickers and scheduling features across Facebook, Instagram and every account.**

For a Live, assign a host, a speaker and a moderator; collect questions beforehand; rehearse sound and connectivity; prepare a backup recording. Do not promise the entire conference stream if only a short public segment is authorized.

Current secondary guidance describes a 60-day account-age requirement and 100 followers for Pages/professional-mode profiles. **[UNVERIFIED] These are the complete requirements applicable to this account on the event date.** Confirm directly in Live Producer before advertising a broadcast. [S28]

One verified operational change is particularly important: Meta's February 2025 policy makes new Facebook Live recordings available for **30 days**, after which they are automatically removed. Download promptly and turn authorized excerpts into reusable content. Facebook should not be the only archive of conference recordings. [S27]

## 7. WhatsApp Channels, Communities, Status and support

### 7.1 Match the product to the relationship

| Product | Documented basis | Recommended event use |
|---|---|---|
| Channel | Meta describes a broadcast surface, separate from personal chats; following does not reveal the follower's phone number to admins or other followers | Public updates, programme announcements and optional reminders [S31] |
| Community | Related groups can be organized together; Meta introduced events and replies in announcement groups | Participant coordination, thematic or regional groups [S32] |
| Group event | Meta documents responses and near-event notifications for people attending | A supplementary meetup/reminder tool, not ticket registration [S32] |
| Status | Meta announced optional Accounts Center sharing from WhatsApp Status to Facebook/Instagram Stories | Voluntary personal invitations by staff and ambassadors [S33] |
| Business messaging | The Business Messaging Policy requires an appropriate opt-in and respecting opt-outs | Individual assistance and expressly requested follow-ups [S34] |

**[UNVERIFIED] Current membership limits, Channel history retention, exact notification defaults, analytics thresholds and regional rollout details.** Older launch announcements are reliable evidence of the product concepts, not proof of every October 2026 limit.

Do not promise privacy identical to a Channel inside a discussion group. Check the actual member/number visibility behavior and explain it before asking people to join. Do not publish private attendee-only details in a public update destination.

### 7.2 A low-effort WhatsApp plan

Recommended starting rhythm:

- Before the final fortnight: one or two genuinely useful Channel updates weekly.
- Final fortnight: publish major changes and real deadlines; avoid simply duplicating the Feed daily.
- Day before: travel/accessibility/contact checklist.
- Event day: a small number of useful updates, with an explicit expectation of higher activity.
- Afterwards: resources, feedback and a clear choice about future updates.

Channel example:

> A programação do [evento] já está disponível. Para quem trabalha com [público], destacamos a conversa sobre [tema], com [palestrante confirmado]. Confira horários e informações de inscrição: [link].

Direct opt-in follow-up example:

> Oi, [nome]. Aqui é [pessoa], da organização do [evento]. Você pediu para receber informações sobre [finalidade]. O [lote/prazo] termina em [data], às [hora de Brasília]. Detalhes: [link]. Se preferir não receber novos avisos, é só responder SAIR.

Do not treat “SAIR” as the only valid withdrawal method; process any clear request to stop. A Channel follow also does not authorize exporting somebody into a separate direct-message marketing list. [S34]

### 7.3 Free app versus paid messaging infrastructure

Start with manual support and native tools already available to the team. **Do not equate the WhatsApp Business app, a Channel, a broadcast feature and the WhatsApp Business Platform/API.** Their rules and costs differ.

The September 2026 Business Messaging Policy says Platform-initiated conversations use approved templates; non-template replies are tied to a 24-hour customer-service window, and messages follow the pricing policy. It also requires a clear escalation path when automation is used. Do not infer that API traffic is free merely because the campaign is organic. **[UNVERIFIED] Current Brazil-specific per-message rates, provider fees and broadcast allowances; not needed for this manual-first plan.** [S34]

Meta One also introduces paid feature tiers for creators and businesses. Use the basic workflow available without purchasing those tiers, and check an actual paywall before teaching a feature as universally free. [S35]

## 8. Posting times, frequency and a manageable calendar

### 8.1 What the studies say—and do not say

| Source | Observed/recommended window | Limit on interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Sprout Social, 2026 Facebook timing guide | Broad strength Tuesday/Wednesday, noon–8 p.m.; education-specific rows often emphasize weekday late morning/afternoon | Global/customer sample; local-time reporting is not a Brazil teacher experiment [S18] |
| Buffer, 14-million-post Facebook analysis | Thursday 9 a.m. singled out; mornings generally strong; Wednesday strongest day overall | Different population and analysis; article/index dates differ across cached variants [S19] |
| Hootsuite, August 2026 schedule guide | Recommends Facebook 1–2 posts daily, while emphasizing brand resources and audience response | An editorial starting point, not a minimum required by Facebook [S20] |

Do not average these schedules into a claimed scientific “best time for Brazil.” Do not convert a source's **local time** as if it were necessarily UTC or US Eastern. Both studies support testing against the account's own results. [S18–S19]

### 8.2 BRT and regional time zones

Use **“horário de Brasília”**, normally **BRT / UTC−3**, in public deadline copy, and select an appropriate location-based time zone such as `America/Sao_Paulo` in tools. Brazil spans multiple time zones. Check the venue and the participating regions, and recheck any daylight-saving decision before future scheduling; the Observatório Nacional explicitly cautions that local rules can change. [S53]

Example for a UTC−3 event: “Inscrições até 12/11, às 23h59, no horário de Brasília.” Do not publish “midnight” without the date and time zone. When the audience spans other Brazilian zones, provide a calendar link or a clear local conversion.

Recommended test windows, all **BRT**, for the first four weeks:

| Test slot | Reason to try it | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Tuesday/Thursday, 07h–09h | Morning hypothesis from global data | Experiment, not proven for teachers |
| Tuesday/Wednesday, 12h–14h | Lunch interval and overlap with Sprout's broad window | Experiment |
| Wednesday/Thursday, 18h30–20h30 | Potential after-work or between-shift availability | Educator-specific hypothesis; **[UNVERIFIED]** |

Rotate comparable topics/formats across windows; do not always put the strongest speaker in one slot. Record performance after a consistent interval, for example 48 hours and seven days. Avoid declaring a winner after two posts. With a small account, qualitative replies and actual registrations may be more useful than noisy percentages.

### 8.3 Recommended workload and cadence

For a team with roughly **5–7 combined hours weekly**, begin with:

- **3–4 Facebook Feed posts weekly**, including 1–2 original Reels and one useful image/multi-image or text post.
- Adapt the strongest 2–3 pieces for Instagram; use a speaker Collab where available.
- Stories on 3–5 days when there is something to show.
- One useful Event-discussion update weekly.
- One or two WhatsApp Channel updates weekly.
- A short daily check for questions, plus a 30-minute weekly review.
- A rehearsed Live every few weeks only if it serves a real audience question.

These quantities are a resource-based recommendation. The team can increase useful updates near a deadline or event day, but no evidence establishes that publishing daily is necessary. Cut a format if it regularly consumes time without delivering useful conversations or registrations.

### 10.4 A/B testing without pretending organic posts are randomized trials

Meta announced native Reels tests for alternative captions/thumbnails in 2023. **[UNVERIFIED] Availability, limits and winner criteria for this team's Page in October 2026.** Check the Professional Dashboard rather than promising the feature. [S39]

If it is unavailable, run modest sequential comparisons:

- Change one factor: question versus benefit opening, teacher versus organizer introduction, short versus longer explanation, or one CTA wording.
- Keep format, audience relevance and measurement window as comparable as possible.
- Record the hypothesis before seeing results.
- Compare several posts, not a single lucky outlier.
- Judge the intended outcome. A deadline post can have fewer comments and still bring more registrations.
- Note confounders: speaker popularity, deadline proximity, partner sharing, weekday and audience overlap.

A useful result is “this angle is worth repeating,” not “this hook increased conversion by 38%” when the sample was tiny and uncontrolled.

### 10.5 Repurpose one real conversation into many useful assets

Recommended workflow from a 15–20-minute authorized speaker interview:

1. Correct the transcript and mark factual claims or third-party material that need checking.
2. Find three genuinely different questions, not three cuts of the same sales pitch.
3. Produce two short captioned clips and one longer explanation if worthwhile.
4. Convert a practical sequence into an Instagram carousel/Facebook multi-image post.
5. Write a text discussion and one Event FAQ answer.
6. Prepare a short WhatsApp digest with the relevant resource/registration link.
7. Draft a speaker kit caption in that speaker's approved voice.
8. Preserve context; let the speaker approve sensitive edits and quotations.

These are distinct adaptations of one owned source, not a reason to flood every surface with duplicates. Track permission, source timecodes, who approved the quote and where it may be reused.

## 12. Pitfalls, privacy and accessibility

### 12.1 Platform-policy and trust risks

Meta has long demoted engagement bait, with the policy expanded to Portuguese. Legitimate requests for advice are different from coercing likes, shares or empty comments to manipulate distribution. The historic policy is reinforced by the 2025 anti-spam initiative; no current universal trigger-word blacklist was verified. [S49, S11]

| Risky tactic | Recommended alternative |
|---|---|
| “Marque cinco professores para concorrer” or forced sharing | Provide an accessible direct registration/resource route; review any promotion separately |
| Purchased likes, engagement pods, coordinated fake comments | Ask real attendees and speakers for voluntary, honest contributions |
| Identical link drops across many groups | Moderator-approved, relevant participation |
| “Últimas vagas” without checking capacity | Give a truthful date/price deadline or verified availability |
| AI-written credentials or testimonials | Use approved biographies and authorized actual statements |
| Fake photos of a successful conference edition | Use real photos, or clearly identified illustrations |

### 12.2 LGPD: a workable approach for leads and WhatsApp numbers

Legal grounding, kept narrow: the LGPD requires an appropriate legal basis and principles such as purpose and necessity; consent is one basis, not the only one. Where relying on consent, it must be demonstrable and tied to specific purposes, with facilitated withdrawal. ANPD's legitimate-interest guide requires a contextual assessment and balancing; legitimate interest is not a blanket marketing exemption and does not apply as that basis to sensitive personal data. [S46–S47]

Recommended implementation for this conference, to be checked against its actual arrangements:

- Separate necessary registration/operational messages from optional future marketing.
- For optional reminders/news, offer unchecked, specific choices for **email** and **WhatsApp**, with an honest frequency/period description.
- Identify the organizer/controller and provide an accessible privacy notice and contact.
- Collect only necessary fields. Do not request a CPF, school details or student information merely to obtain a downloadable schedule.
- Record the wording, purpose, channel, timestamp and source of each opt-in.
- Honor opt-outs promptly and keep only the minimum suppression record needed to avoid contacting the person again.
- Define access and retention; do not leave attendee exports in a public shared folder.
- Do not transfer the list to sponsors/partners for unrelated marketing without an appropriate basis and transparent arrangement.
- Do not upload identifiable registration exports into general AI tools to “find better audiences.”

Suggested optional wording, requiring organizer review:

> [ ] Quero receber pelo WhatsApp informações e lembretes sobre [evento/edição] até [período], em média [frequência]. Posso cancelar a qualquer momento. Consulte o aviso de privacidade: [link].

Use a separate choice for future editions or broader newsletters when that is a different purpose. Declining optional marketing should not prevent the required registration process. These are recommended design choices, not a claim that one checkbox alone makes the whole operation compliant.

For WhatsApp Business specifically, the current policy independently requires the recipient's supplied number/username and opt-in permission for subsequent messages/calls, plus honoring requests to stop. A public phone number, group membership or Facebook Event response is not a sufficient substitute for this permission. [S34]

Accessibility requests may reveal sensitive information. Ask how to support participation, limit access to those arranging the accommodation and avoid diagnoses in marketing spreadsheets. For minors, school photographs or student stories, arrange the necessary institutional/legal review and permissions before publication. **[UNVERIFIED] The actual event's legal basis, controller/operator roles, retention periods and minor-participation procedures.**

### 12.3 Analytics and cookies

ANPD's cookie guide recommends clear choices, easily visible rejection of non-necessary cookies and consent-based cookies disabled by default. Do not assume all analytics collection is exempt from privacy requirements. For this small project, using existing platform reports may avoid unnecessary tracking complexity; if adding GA4, document the purposes and configure the applicable consent/privacy arrangement. Do not install advertising pixels merely because they appear in a ticketing tutorial. [S48]

### 12.4 Accessibility is part of the content workflow

W3C guidance explains that images need alternatives conveying their information/function and that captions include relevant speech **and non-speech audio**. Automatic captions require checking; a transcript alone does not provide synchronized captions. [S51–S52]

Recommended publishing checklist:

- [ ] Put essential date, location, time zone, price and registration instructions in accessible text, not only the graphic.
- [ ] Add/edit alt text where the publishing surface permits; verify after cross-posting.
- [ ] If no alt-text control exists for that surface, include an appropriate image description in the caption and use an accessible linked page.
- [ ] Correct names, acronyms and Portuguese speech in video captions.
- [ ] Identify speakers and important sound cues when needed for understanding.
- [ ] Use readable type, sufficient contrast and uncluttered frames; check on a small phone.
- [ ] Avoid relying on color alone to distinguish programme tracks or deadlines.
- [ ] Read out essential visual-only information during videos; provide suitable description/transcript when needed.
- [ ] Use descriptive links and restrained emojis; avoid decorative Unicode fonts.
- [ ] Provide real accessibility information and a human contact. Mention Libras only if confirmed.

Example alt text, **illustrative**: “Professora apresenta três perguntas sobre avaliação em uma sala de formação. A arte informa: [nome do evento], [data], [cidade].” Replace placeholders and describe what is actually present; do not infer identities or disabilities.
