# CONAFROEDU 2026: conference context, Brazilian academic marketing, and responsible representation

**Research date:** 21 October 2026, America/Denver. **Campaign reference date:** 21 October 2026. **Scope:** evidence and recommendations for a small volunteer committee marketing a paid, national, online academic congress through Facebook-first organic communication. No outreach was sent and no accounts or event settings were changed.

**Pass 2 verification:** independently re-searched on 21 October 2026; reopened the live Even3 page in a rendered browser and re-inspected both deadline graphics. Corrections are incorporated below. New practical material appears in §§5.4, 7.4–7.5, and 8.6–8.8; the final verification log distinguishes confirmed claims from access limitations and unresolved organizer decisions.

## 1. Evidence, confidence, and the most important findings

Read first: `docs/BRIEF.md`, particularly “THE ACTUAL CONFERENCE,” and `docs/conference-announcement.md`. These establish the project brief and supplied promotional claims. Public research below supplements them; it does not certify the organizers, academic quality, affiliations, or eventual delivery of advertised benefits.

Evidence labels used throughout:

- **Verified public listing:** observed on a first-party event, institutional, government, publisher, or platform page on the research date. A published promise is evidence of what is advertised, not proof that it has already been delivered.
- **Supplied announcement:** supplied by the project, sometimes corroborated publicly.
- **Recommendation / inference:** an editorial or operational proposal derived from the evidence, not a measured result or an organizer decision.
- **[UNVERIFIED]:** missing, conflicting, inaccessible, or not independently established. Do not silently convert these items into public-facing facts.

### Immediate implications for the website

1. **The submission campaign has only two weeks left.** The live Even3 submission section and two organizer graphics give **4 November 2026** as the deadline. The congress itself is **5–6 December**. A generic six-week campaign that keeps asking for papers through late November would be wrong. [Event](https://www.even3.com.br/iconafroedu-764690/), [submission graphic](https://static.even3.com/pagina-evento/1001001484.dada13e93fdc4ba7b83a.png), [calendar graphic](https://static.even3.com/pagina-evento/1001001426.093a8a526e0446bea557.png).
2. **20 November should support attendance and pedagogical engagement.** It falls after the currently published paper deadline. Connect Novembro Negro to year-round anti-racist practice, with a registration CTA appropriate to the actual phase.
3. **Use displayed ticket prices, not search metadata.** The rendered ticket page shows prices different from the lower values in its structured metadata. The browser-rendered prices are recorded below; the final checkout total remains [UNVERIFIED].
4. **Publication has an additional advertised charge.** The event separately lists **R$ 16,98 per work** for proceedings publication. Do not imply that a ticket mentioning one submission necessarily includes that charge.
5. **Presentation requirements conflict.** Rules and slide instructions say presentations are optional; the FAQ says they are mandatory. The committee should resolve this before AI writes submission FAQs or automated responses.
6. **AI representation is part of the event’s credibility.** Real participant photography with permission, credited Black Brazilian creative work, and clear typography are strong defaults. Generated people, cultural symbols, religious imagery, and historical scenes require human review; adding “diverse” to a prompt is insufficient.

### 3.1 Cost details worth teaching accurately

The [official marketing features page](https://plataforma.even3.com.br/solucoes/divulgacao-de-eventos/) advertises no extra charge for its marketing tools, free use for free registrations, and a **10% charge on paid registrations**. This is general platform information; who absorbs charges and the event’s final checkout terms must be checked. Proceedings/publication services and a custom app are separate questions.

The [current certificate-pricing help](https://ajuda.even3.com.br/hc/pt-br/articles/360061673691-Quanto-custa-o-m%C3%B3dulo-de-certificados-da-Even3) says the certificate module has no platform charge for paid events. For entirely free events it describes a 90-day download window and a later **R$ 19,90** charge unless the organizer covers it. Search excerpts still showed **R$ 12,90**; the live article/API updated on 21 October 2026 shows R$ 19,90. **Do not apply the free-event charge to CONAFROEDU**, which lists paid tickets, or announce a separate certificate fee without an event-specific basis.

### 4.3 Academic value propositions, with honest qualifications

| Audience / value | Useful framing | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Basic-education teachers | Exchange classroom practices, curriculum approaches, and ERER implementation experiences | “Elimine o racismo da sua escola em dois dias” or promises that the event satisfies all professional training obligations. |

| Postgraduate students and researchers | Present and discuss ongoing/completed work; proceedings, accurate bibliographic records, thematic networking | Guaranteed selection-process points, journal equivalence, or automatic PPG recognition. |
| School managers | Reflection on school response, curriculum, teacher development, and community participation | Promising a PNEERQ credential, accreditation, or MEC approval without documented authorization. |
| Community educators / social movements | Recognition of situated practices, collective knowledge, territory, and educational experience | Framing community knowledge as raw material for outsiders or implying academic credentials are the only valid expertise. |
| Publication | ISBN identifies a publication; DOI supports persistent identification and discovery | “ISBN/DOI garante qualidade”, automatic indexation, a journal impact factor, or guaranteed citations. |

**Qualis needs a 2026 update.** ISBN/DOI does not establish a Qualis rating. CAPES announced on **23 May 2025** that the **2025–2028** evaluation shifts toward classification of articles, with procedures selected/combined by evaluation area. Historical Qualis data may still matter in particular institutional rules, but the guide should not market proceedings as automatically “Qualis A” or promise points. Verify the relevant area, current notice, and actual publication vehicle. [CAPES announcement](https://www.gov.br/capes/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/avaliacao-da-producao-intelectual-e-ampliada).

Similarly, professional progression depends on employer/career rules and the participant’s circumstances. “Certificado de 40 horas” is an advertised event offer; “garantia de progressão funcional” is not.

### 4.4 Deadline extension: recognizable convention, not a script to fake

**Observed:** COPENE’s 2026 bulletin extended full-work delivery to **31 July 2026** and specified the participant area as the submission route. This is evidence that **prorrogação de prazo** is a recognizable academic communication format, not proof that every event should extend deadlines. [First-party bulletin](https://www.copene2026.abpn.org.br/informativo/view?ID_INFORMATIVO=30&TIPO=33).

**Recommendations:**

- Announce an extension only after the scientific committee decides it and the live platform is updated.
- Specify **which** deadline changes: abstracts, full papers, corrected files, videos, publication payment, or registrations.
- Show the previous and new dates, the cutoff time/timezone if confirmed, and any downstream effects.
- Preserve fair treatment for people who complied with the earlier deadline.
- Avoid a preplanned series of false “última chance” claims, invented demand, “a pedidos” without evidence, or fake limited capacity for an online event.
- Correct scheduled posts, the pinned Facebook invitation, WhatsApp forwardable text, email templates, graphics, and FAQs together.
- If no extension occurs, stop submission CTAs after 4 November and invite people as participants/listeners according to the available categories.

### 5.2 The education laws: relevance and correct anniversaries

- **Lei 10.639, 9 January 2003:** changed the LDB to require Afro-Brazilian history and culture in fundamental and secondary education, and included 20 November in the school calendar. Its **23rd anniversary was 9 January 2026**, not November. [Official text](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2003/l10.639.htm).
- **Lei 11.645, 10 March 2008:** amended the curriculum requirement to include Afro-Brazilian **and Indigenous** history and culture in public and private fundamental and secondary education, across the curriculum, especially arts, literature, and history. Its **18th anniversary was 10 March 2026**. [Official text](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2007-2010/2008/lei/l11645.htm).
- **Lei 14.759, 21 December 2023:** established **20 November** as the national holiday for Dia Nacional de Zumbi e da Consciência Negra. Do not say the observance itself originated in 2023 or that its holiday status is only municipal/state-based. [Official text](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2023/lei/l14759.htm).

**Recommended phrasing:** “Como as Leis nº 10.639/2003 e nº 11.645/2008 se traduzem no currículo e nas práticas da sua escola?” This is a discussion invitation, not a promise of legal compliance through attendance. Do not collapse Black and Indigenous histories into one interchangeable identity.

### 5.3 PNEERQ: current policy context, not invented endorsement

The **Política Nacional de Equidade, Educação para as Relações Étnico-Raciais e Educação Escolar Quilombola (PNEERQ)** was instituted by **Portaria MEC 470, 14 May 2024**; the government's reference page also identifies the amendment **Portaria MEC 1.082, 29 October 2024**. Its relevance is ongoing policy implementation, professional formation, racial equity, and quilombola education. It is a policy instituted by ministerial ordinance, not a new 2026 law. [MIR overview, updated 13 October 2026](https://www.gov.br/igualdaderacial/pt-br/assuntos/programas-e-projetos/observatorio-de-monitoramento-e-avaliacao/politicas-intergovernamentais/educacao/perguntas-e-respostas/politica-nacional-de-equidade-educacao-para-as-relacoes-etnico-raciais-e-educacao-escolar-quilombola-pneerq).

MEC's reference document describes seven areas: governance; diagnosis/monitoring; formation of managers and teachers; teaching/literary materials; prevention/response protocols; affirmation of Black and quilombola trajectories; and dissemination of knowledge. These provide specific editorial topics. **Adhesion is through education authorities/SIMEC**, not an individual congress registration. SECADI/MEC coordinates the policy; the FNDE page separately explains governance-agent scholarships. Neither attending CONAFROEDU nor receiving its certificate establishes PNEERQ accreditation. [MEC reference document, p. 29](https://www.gov.br/mec/pt-br/escola-em-tempo-integral/documentos-arquivos/Docreferncia.pdf), [FNDE responsibilities and scholarship access](https://www.gov.br/fnde/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao/acoes-e-programas/programas/bolsas-e-auxilios/pneerq-politica-nacional-de-equidade-educacao-para-as-relacoes-etnico-raciais-e-educacao-escolar-quilombola). Direct MEC retrieval sometimes presents a human-verification page; MIR/FNDE were accessible and MEC's document was searchable in the index. This is not a complete legal consolidation of every later amendment.

**Verified 2026 development:** MEC reporting published **2 September 2026** discusses the **Diagnóstico Equidade 2026** and increased adoption of formal response protocols. The article’s headline says “schools,” but its body reports **education networks**: state networks from 59% to 93%, municipal networks from 36% to 53%. If using the figures, retain that denominator and identify them as MEC-reported findings; do not call them a percentage of every individual Brazilian school. [Agência Gov, via MEC](https://agenciagov.ebc.com.br/noticias/202609/redes-adotam-protocolos-para-responder-ao-racismo-na-escola).

The same report emphasizes staff training, protection of students, pedagogical measures, and ongoing follow-up. A useful conference post could ask what educators need to put institutional protocols into practice. Do not imply that CONAFROEDU training is an official PNEERQ course or that MEC is a partner.

MEC also published a **July 2026 extension of participation in the Diagnóstico Equidade**. This is a real 2026 policy-implementation item, but the relevant deadline is historical by this campaign. The article’s current body was blocked by a human-verification page during direct retrieval; use it only as a linked historical lead, not a still-open action. [MEC article](https://www.gov.br/mec/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/2026/julho/mec-amplia-prazo-de-participacao-no-diagnostico-de-equidade-racial).

MEC open-data material says a national quilombola education program was planned for 2026. **[UNVERIFIED]** launch status and operative details as of 21 October; do not announce a launch from forecast wording alone. [MEC open-data page](https://dadosabertos.mec.gov.br/pneerq-escola-quilombo).

### 8.2 Brazilian perspectives: more than correcting a prompt

**Tarcízio Silva** studies how digital systems reproduce structural racial inequalities. His first-party profile identifies his book **Racismo Algorítmico** (2022), **Griots e Tecnologias Digitais** (2023), and the 2025 print edition of **Inteligência Artificial Generativa: discriminação e impactos sociais**. These are useful Brazilian frameworks for asking whose knowledge is extracted, who has authority, who reviews the output, and who bears the harm. [Profile](https://tarciziosilva.com.br/blog/sobre/), [publications](https://tarciziosilva.com.br/blog/publicacoes-e-artigos/).

An openly available version of the generative-AI collection is hosted by [Ação Educativa](https://acaoeducativa.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Inteligencia-Artificial-Generativa-discriminacao-e-impactos-sociais_compressed.pdf). The URL’s 2024 directory is not, by itself, proof of the PDF’s edition date. Do not merge its bibliographic details with the 2025 print edition without checking the title page.

The UECE hair/beauty study also points readers toward Brazilian research by **Fernanda Carrera** on racialized image banks. Its directly verified finding is about white/straight-haired visibility in stock search; this supports reviewing both generated and purchased stock visuals. Do not mislabel all algorithmic racism research as image-generation research.

**Practical implication / recommendation:** give Black educators, designers, and relevant cultural knowledge-holders meaningful decision power over the campaign. They should be able to reject an attractive but misleading image. Prompt refinement cannot substitute for consent, source verification, institutional accountability, or payment for creative work when commissioned.

### 8.3 Specific representational failure modes to inspect

| Risk | What can go wrong | Practical response |
|---|---|---|
| Colorism | Light-skinned people dominate prestigious roles while dark-skinned people appear mainly as needy students or in hardship scenes. | Review the whole campaign together: who teaches, leads, speaks, researches, and receives care? Include dark-skinned Black people in varied authoritative and ordinary roles. |
| Hair and “beautification” | Straightening, loosening curls, replacing protective styles, changing hairlines, or making dark skin lighter during portrait enhancement. | Prefer original approved photos. Compare edits side by side; specify preservation of identity and texture, then inspect the result. |
| Homogenized faces | Many supposedly different people share the same face, features, age, body type, or styling. | Review variation, age, gender, disability, and regional context without forcing a demographic checklist into every single image. |
| Generic Africa | Savannahs, animals, huts, masks, and invented “tribal” clothing used for a contemporary Brazilian education event. | Specify a real place/context and the educational activity; include contemporary classrooms, research, books, digital participation, urban and rural life without hierarchy. |
| Cultural collage | Unrelated cloth, scripts, masks, ornaments, and religious objects combined as an “African aesthetic.” | Identify the actual culture and source for each element. If no real reason exists to use a symbol, use typography and ordinary geometric composition. |
| Erasure of knowledge | Black people pictured as recipients while white people appear as experts or controllers of technology. | Check spatial position, size, gaze, action, microphone ownership, and who explains the lesson. |
| Poverty or trauma default | Every Black/African educational scene becomes a story of deprivation, slavery, suffering, or rescue. | Include scholarship, creativity, collective agency, joy, work, disagreement, and ordinary life. Do not erase historical violence, but use it only when substantively relevant. |
| False documentation | Synthetic classrooms, audiences, prior editions, testimonials, or speakers look like real evidence. | Never use invented participation as social proof. Mark illustration and AI use clearly; use actual, permitted documentation for factual claims. |
| Video drift | Skin shade, facial identity, hair, clothing, symbols, or accent changes between frames; a “neutral” voice becomes caricature. | Review the full video with sound, including opening/closing frames and captions; use consented speaker audio or text-based motion graphics. |
| Historical invention | AI invents a historical figure’s portrait, quote, biography, event, or garment and presents it as an archive. | Use verified sources and genuine archival material with appropriate rights. Label interpretive illustration; do not manufacture quotations. |

These are editorial review categories grounded in the research, not measured frequencies for all tools. Never use a model or automated classifier to “verify” the race, religion, or quilombola identity of real people from appearance.

### 8.4 Candomblé, Umbanda, orixás, Adinkra, Kente, and Ubuntu

**Evidence gap:** this scan did not establish a robust 2023–2026 controlled benchmark specifically measuring generator accuracy for Afro-Brazilian orixás, Candomblé, Umbanda, and Adinkra. The safeguards below combine cultural-source evidence with editorial judgment. Do not invent a study or a numerical error rate for these traditions.

- **Religious diversity:** Candomblé and Umbanda are not interchangeable labels or a single standardized iconography. A congress on Africanidades is not necessarily a religious congress, and Black identity does not establish an individual’s religion. The Brazilian policy for traditional terreiro and African-matrix communities emphasizes respect for their cultures, knowledge, practices, and participation. [MIR policy](https://www.gov.br/igualdaderacial/pt-br/assuntos/programas-e-projetos/politica-nacional-para-povos-e-comunidades-tradicionais-de-terreiro-e-matriz-africana/politica-nacional-para-povos-e-comunidades-tradicionais-de-terreiro-e-matriz-africana).
- **Sacred imagery:** do not use orixás, ritual clothing, sacred objects, or invented ceremonies as generic decorative backgrounds. If a specific session requires them, consult an appropriate practitioner/knowledge-holder about names, attributes, context, permissibility, and what must not be depicted. One reviewer cannot speak for every terreiro or tradition. Avoid whitewashing, demonizing, sexualizing, or conflating sacred figures with fantasy characters.
- **Adinkra:** Ghana’s cultural sources describe a meaningful symbolic language associated with Akan traditions; it is not a collection of arbitrary universal “African icons.” Verify the symbol’s name, form, context, and interpretation through knowledgeable sources. Do not ask AI to invent a glyph and assign it a scholarly meaning. [Ghana Ministry of Tourism, Culture & Creative Arts](https://www.motcca.gov.gh/traditional-symbols-in-ghana-adinkra/), [National Commission on Culture](https://ghanaculture.gov.gh/world-recognition-of-adinkra/).
- **Kente:** UNESCO describes Ghanaian strip-weaving knowledge, skills, and social context, inscribed in **2024**. Do not label any colorful zigzag a Kente pattern or represent it as Brazil’s single ancestral cloth. Avoid assuming that heritage is permission to copy a contemporary artist’s design. [UNESCO listing](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/craftsmanship-of-traditional-woven-textile-kente-02130).
- **Ubuntu:** a named CONAFROEDU program item makes it relevant, but do not use an AI-generated one-line slogan as a complete account of African philosophy, or imply it is a concept shared identically across all African cultures. Ask the actual speaker to approve an explanation and quotation.
- **Current branding:** existing event graphics include generalized African visual motifs. Their presence is not blanket authorization to invent further symbols, traditions, or cultural explanations. A visual audit can refine future assets without making unsupported claims about the original artist’s intent.

For broader storytelling practice, [Africa No Filter’s ethical storytelling resources](https://africanofilter.org/what-we-do/advocacy/) are an African-led reference. Its [2021 ethical-storytelling handbook launch](https://africanofilter.org/press-office/press-releases/new-handbook-outlines-how-to-write-about-africa-for-development-community/) explicitly addresses white-savior framing, co-creation, meaningful consent, local knowledge, and subject review. The [2022 journalists' guide launch](https://africanofilter.org/press-office/press-releases/new-handbook-outlines-how-journalists-can-write-about-africa-beyond-stereotypes/) supports specificity, lived expertise, and agency. These are distinct publications. The ethical handbook's [current PDF location](https://africanofilter.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/How-to-tell-an-african-story.pdf) is a 2025 upload path, **not its launch date**. Apply those principles to Brazil with Brazilian expertise; the handbook does not claim to be a specialist Afro-Brazilian religious guide.

### 8.6 Human review checklist for every image or video

**Stop publication if a material item cannot be answered.** This is a proposed workflow for the guide, not a platform requirement.

**Grounding:** the checklist operationalizes the Brazilian stock-search findings in [Câmara/Silva](https://revistas.uece.br/index.php/linguagememfoco/article/view/16028), [Tarcízio Silva's structural analysis](https://tarciziosilva.com.br/blog/sobre/), African-led [ethical-storytelling guidance](https://africanofilter.org/press-office/press-releases/new-handbook-outlines-how-to-write-about-africa-for-development-community/), and the [MIR terreiro/community policy](https://www.gov.br/igualdaderacial/pt-br/assuntos/programas-e-projetos/politica-nacional-para-povos-e-comunidades-tradicionais-de-terreiro-e-matriz-africana/politica-nacional-para-povos-e-comunidades-tradicionais-de-terreiro-e-matriz-africana). The [Rede JP/Peregum manual page](https://redejpcomunicacao.org/manual-antirracista/) provides a Brazilian professional communication reference on racial representations and inclusive practice; its download requests form details, and no form was submitted. This checklist is the researcher's application of those principles, not a quotation or a validated bias-removal method.

- [ ] The message serves a defined conference audience and relates to the actual program.
- [ ] Every factual name, date, price, law number, title, and CTA matches the approved fact sheet.
- [ ] The asset does not imply an existing partnership, endorsement, prior edition, or testimonial without evidence.
- [ ] Real people’s photos, voices, and likenesses have permission for this use; no unauthorized face or voice cloning.
- [ ] Any generated people are clearly illustrative, not presented as actual attendees or speakers.
- [ ] Black people appear with agency, varied expertise, and appropriate prominence.
- [ ] Darker skin is represented without linking it systematically to hardship or subordinate roles.
- [ ] An edit has not lightened skin, changed facial features, or altered hair texture/identity.
- [ ] Lighting/exposure preserves detail in dark skin without whitening it, turning every face the same shade, or hiding facial detail in shadow. Compare real portraits against the approved original, not an AI “beauty” score.
- [ ] Hair choices are concrete and plausible: natural tightly coiled/crespo hair, cacheado textures, braids/tranças, locs, short cuts, shaved heads, or straightened hair where chosen by the person. No single style is treated as the authentic or professional way to be Black.
- [ ] Faces, bodies, hands, devices, hair, assistive technology, and classroom objects are credible.
- [ ] The setting is specific and appropriate; no automatic savannah/hut/animal shorthand.
- [ ] The composition avoids white-savior and poverty defaults: Black educators explain, research, create, and use technology; classroom infrastructure follows the real context rather than a racial stereotype.
- [ ] No symbol, garment, script, word, or religious object is used without an understood reason and source.
- [ ] Relevant practitioners/knowledge-holders reviewed sacred or culturally specific material.
- [ ] The work does not merge Black, African, Afro-Brazilian, Indigenous, and quilombola identities as equivalents.
- [ ] Historical interpretation is distinguished from documentary evidence.
- [ ] The complete video was watched with sound; identity, skin, hair, voice, symbols, and captions remain appropriate throughout.
- [ ] Portuguese names, diacritics, pronunciation, and captions have been checked by a person.
- [ ] Essential details also appear as readable text; alt text describes the image without guessing sensitive identities.
- [ ] Contrast, text size, caption timing, and mobile readability are adequate.
- [ ] AI disclosure and creative/photo credits are clear where applicable.
- [ ] The image's creator, source URL, permitted promotional use, credit requirements, and any relevant subject permission are recorded. “Free,” “educational,” and “organic post” are not interchangeable licenses.
- [ ] A named human reviewer has authority to request replacement, and the final approved version is identifiable.

**Practical review order:** check the caption/facts, inspect the image at full size, compare any portrait edits to originals, then view the entire campaign grid for skin-tone/authority patterns. Ask a Black Brazilian educator/designer with agreed time and decision-making authority to review representation; use a relevant practitioner for a specific religious tradition. One person is not a representative of every community. If a concern cannot be resolved, switch to an approved photo or typography rather than repeatedly generating a plausible-looking symbol.

### 8.7 Example prompts for the guide

These are **draft prompt patterns**, not generated assets or cultural guarantees.

**Lower-risk starting point: typography / motion design**

> Crie um storyboard de 15 segundos para divulgar o CONAFROEDU 2026 a professoras e professores brasileiros. Use tipografia legível e movimento de formas geométricas simples. Fatos: 5 e 6 de dezembro de 2026; 100% on-line; inscrições na Even3. Não invente pessoas, falas, símbolos culturais, imagens de edições anteriores ou apoiadores. Deixe a carga horária e as condições de certificação para inserção após conferência da organização. Entregue o roteiro, a ordem das telas e uma lista dos fatos que precisam de revisão humana.

**Contemporary illustrative scene, if an image is needed**

> Ilustração editorial, claramente não documental, de professoras e professores negros brasileiros adultos preparando juntos uma atividade de educação antirracista em uma escola contemporânea. Mostre colaboração e protagonismo intelectual; inclua diferentes tons de pele e penteados sem uniformizar os rostos. Ambiente e roupas cotidianos, materiais pedagógicos plausíveis e espaço livre para texto. Não use símbolos religiosos nem estampas apresentadas como pertencentes a uma cultura específica. Não represente esta cena como fotografia do CONAFROEDU. A imagem será revisada por uma educadora negra antes de publicação.

**Review prompt, after human consent and with no private information**

> Analise esta peça como apoio à revisão: quem está no centro, quem ensina, quem escuta e quem usa a tecnologia? Há uniformização dos rostos, associação entre tom de pele e posição de autoridade, elementos culturais sem contexto ou aparência de registro documental? Liste dúvidas e possíveis problemas; não infira raça ou religião de pessoas reais e não declare a peça culturalmente correta. A aprovação é humana.

The model can help surface questions; it cannot certify respectful representation. For real speaker portraits, use the approved original and ordinary layout changes wherever possible.

**More specific Afro-Brazilian illustration pattern: roles, skin, hair, and context**

> Ilustração editorial contemporânea, sem aparência de fotografia documental. Três docentes negros brasileiros adultos discutem juntos um plano de aula de história e cultura afro-brasileira em uma escola brasileira atual. Uma professora de pele retinta apresenta o planejamento e um professor de pele castanha organiza as referências; a terceira docente faz perguntas e anota. Varie os traços faciais e os tons de pele, com iluminação que preserve seus detalhes. Inclua cabelo crespo curto, tranças e cabelo cacheado, desenhados com cuidado; são escolhas destes personagens, não uniformes de uma identidade. Roupas cotidianas, biblioteca e materiais escolares plausíveis, computador utilizado pelos próprios docentes. Composição horizontal com espaço para texto inserido depois. Sem cenário de resgate, pobreza como atalho visual, salvador branco, estampa “tribal” genérica, animal exótico ou objeto religioso. Não associe a cena a participantes reais do CONAFROEDU. Não gere letras, nomes ou logotipos. A legenda dirá “Ilustração gerada com IA; não retrata participantes do evento”.

This is a fictional scene; reject outputs that still homogenize faces or move authority away from the specified educator. Vary roles across subsequent assets instead of repeating one visual hierarchy. Mentioning hair types does not justify altering a real person's chosen style.

**Authorized portrait layout: preserve identity**

> Use somente o retrato aprovado e autorizado de [nome] para este layout. Preserve o tom de pele, os traços do rosto, a textura e o volume do cabelo, idade e marcas pessoais. Ajuste apenas enquadramento e exposição de modo natural; reserve uma área externa à foto para a identificação. Não alise cabelo, afine traços, clareie pele ou reconstrua o rosto. Compare com a imagem original e sinalize se o pedido exigir alteração de identidade. O texto será inserido e conferido separadamente.

Prefer a conventional crop/layout tool for that task. Authorization for publishing a portrait does not automatically authorize uploading it to an external AI service.

**Culturally specific session: research and consultation before drawing**

> Precisamos divulgar uma mesa sobre [tradição/comunidade específica, confirmada pela pessoa convidada]. ## 9. Brazilian academic-audience copy conventions

### 9.1 Tone and vocabulary

**Recommendation:** academic but welcoming, community-minded, specific, and accessible. Address teachers as professionals with experience to share. Explain a technical term at first use. Avoid literal English marketing translations, inflated credentials, and an urgency tone that overwhelms the educational purpose.

| Term / convention | Appropriate use | Important distinction |
|---|---|---|
| **Chamada de trabalhos** / **chamada para submissão de trabalhos** | Formal invitation stating scope, modalities, dates, rules, and review | An **edital** suggests a formal document; do not call a social caption an edital if no such document exists. |
| **Inscrição** | A person’s event registration | Different from submitting a work or paying a publication fee. |
| **Submissão** | Sending a work for evaluation | Does not mean acceptance, presentation, or publication. |
| **Parecer**, **avaliação**, **carta de aceite** | Scientific review and its documented result | Do not describe AI-generated praise as a scientific review. |
| **Eixos / áreas temáticas** | Organize submissions by actual event categories | Copy the live category name accurately; do not invent a thematic track. |
| **Artigo completo**, **resumo expandido**, **resumo simples** | Distinct submission formats | Page limits and structure depend on this event’s rules. |
| **Relato de experiência / práticas exitosas** | Reflective account of educational practice | Do not imply that effectiveness has been independently proven or that consent/ethics can be skipped. |
| **Anais** | Proceedings collecting event work | Not automatically a journal, book chapter, or Qualis-rated publication. |
| **Ouvinte / participante**, **autor(a)**, **apresentador(a)** | Different participation roles | Different roles may have different requirements/certificates. |
| **Carga horária**, **certificado de participação** | State documented hours and conditions | Not a diploma, professional license, MEC recognition, or guaranteed credit. |
| **Prorrogação de prazo**, **virada de lote** | Real changes to specific deadlines or prices | Keep scientific and commercial milestones separate. |
| **ERER** | Educação para as Relações Étnico-Raciais, explained at first mention | Do not expect an unfamiliar teacher to know every policy acronym. |
| **Pessoas negras**, **população negra**, **culturas afro-brasileiras** | Use contextual, respectful wording and self-identification | Avoid euphemisms such as “moreno” to label someone, or treating “negro,” “africano,” and “quilombola” as synonyms. |

Brazilian calls often use **pesquisadoras e pesquisadores**, **professoras e professores**, or an institution’s established inclusive style. Use a consistent readable form and accessible sentence structure. Avoid inventing a universal required gender style or assuming every audience wants the same form.

Conventions that improve clarity:

- Write **4 de novembro de 2026** in prose; use **04/11/2026** in compact calendars. In English, write **4 November 2026** to avoid month/day ambiguity.
- Give **horário de Brasília (UTC−3)** when the committee confirms the exact cutoff/session time. Do not use the researcher’s America/Denver timezone in participant-facing copy.
- Format money as **R$ 55,00** and hours as **40 horas**. Avoid “40h de aulas ao vivo” unless that duration is actually documented.
- Put the main action first: submit a work **or** register as a participant. Link to the relevant instructions and a single authoritative registration page.
- Use the event’s full name once, then CONAFROEDU; preserve Portuguese names and diacritics.
- Few purposeful emojis can work in social posts. A formal list/email call should remain readable without relying on icons or all caps.
- Limit hashtags to relevant discoverability; supplied tags are starting points, not proven growth tactics.

### 9.4 CONAFROEDU submission formatting and ABNT

The public [event rules](https://www.even3.com.br/iconafroedu-764690/) currently specify:

| Format | Published length | Structural emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Artigo completo | Up to **20 pages** | Research question/context, method, results/discussion, conclusion, and references, with title/authors/abstract/keywords. |
| Resumo expandido | **2–5 pages** | Objective, approach, results, conclusion, keywords, and references. |
| Resumo simples | **1 page** | Concise structured research account; present in the public rules/live module despite omission from the supplied announcement. |
| Práticas exitosas / relato de experiência | Up to **10 pages** | Context, development of the practice, results, reflection, and references. |

General settings displayed: **PDF; A4; Arial or Times New Roman 12; 1.5 line spacing; justified text; top/left margins 3 cm and bottom/right 2 cm; up to six authors; ABNT citations and references**. The responsible submitting author must be registered. The page emphasizes using the same email for registration and submission.

Do not present these settings as universal ABNT requirements for every paper. They are the event’s chosen submission instructions. Anonymous-file requirements, template download, word limits for each abstract, whether references count toward the page limit, and treatment of tables/figures are **[UNVERIFIED]** unless clarified in the actual submission form or an approved template.

**Current ABNT context:** university libraries identify **NBR 10520:2023** for citations and **NBR 6023:2025** for references; **NBR 14724:2024** concerns academic-work presentation. Some older AI answers still default to NBR 6023:2018. Use the committee’s current template and a university library guide; do not claim an outdated citation/reference template is current. [UFES library](https://biblioteca.ufes.br/manuais-de-normalizacao), [ECA-USP library](https://www.eca.usp.br/biblioteca/normalizacao).

**Safe AI role:** turn the actual rules into a checklist; identify missing sections; suggest clearer wording; format references from verified metadata. The author must check every quotation, source, DOI, result, author attribution, and ethics requirement. Do not have AI invent references, results, participant consent, or “ABNT-compliant” guarantees, and do not upload another author’s unpublished work without authorization.
